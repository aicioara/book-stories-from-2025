# My relationship with Facebook

Facebook, now rebranded Meta, is a fascinating company. It is one of those products that delivered immense value to me, and also a company which I knew very well. For that reason, I always defended it, despite the popular backlash. I sided with them in the Cambridge Analytica scandal and I was on their side on the Ads argument: I understood that Facebook does not actually sell my data, instead they monetize my data by showing me ads, but the buyer of the ad space does not in fact get my data.

With that said, my entire opinion of them changed this year, so in this article, I will talk about my relationship with Facebook, how it benefited me and what changed.

## Beginnings

I created my Facebook account on June 23, 2010, when a friend of mine from the World of Warcraft community called Martin Ivanov (I believe from Bulgaria) asked me to join the network. No one in Romania was using it at the time, so I only had this one friend. The app was not very useful to me.

In January 2011, my classmates started using Facebook, so I checked my account again and started adding people to my friends list. Back then, we would use Yahoo Messenger to communicate and hi5.com to post pictures and the chat that Facebook provided was really quite terrible. However, the "wall" where you shared pictures and the ability to comment was better than hi5, so we  moved our picture sharing to Facebook, while keeping our chats on Yahoo Messenger.

One feature that I had on Yahoo Messenger was the ability to put people in certain categories. I had my classmates, my close friends, my neighbors, my gaming friends, etc. I was super organized and I liked it that way. Facebook did not have this concept, but I did not realize at the time, so I created the "classmates" group and I added all of my classmates there. That is how I basically became the admin of the group chat for my high school class.

In 2012, I left Romania and moved to the UK. Facebook was crucial to maintaining relationships with my old classmates. Posting pictures, commenting and DM-ing people made these virtual relationships so real, that when I'd go back home, after months of being in the UK, the conversations would flow naturally, almost as if no time has passed at all and no distance existed prior. It was amazing.

Around 2013, Facebook had an innovative "Open Graph" protocol that you could use. You would be able to ask questions such as "Friends of mine, who were born in my hometown, who went to the same university as I am and who are currently in Berlin" and it would come with a list that matched all criteria. I met many of people that way, that I would not have otherwise met.

In 2014, I saw an ad for a Real Estate seminar. It was one of those things where they wanted to upsell you the full course, but the basics that they presented were mind blowing to me at the time. Facebook and their ads are how I learned about Real Estate.

In 2015, I moved to the United States for 6 months and I started sharing on Facebook weekly updates that got longer and longer in size. That is how I got into blogging, which got me into journaling, which eventually led to this article.

When I moved to California in 2015, I did not have any friends. I used Facebook to search and find a bachata dancing group, which became my family during the 6 months there. Without Facebook, I could never have found Omid, which eventually accepted me into their dance group.

All in all, Facebook was a net positive to me.

## Break in functionality

Sometime in 2016, I decided that I really wanted to focus on my final university exams and I installed a browser extension called "Never get distracted by Facebook". What this extension did was hide the news feed. Basically still allow you to use the app, go to groups, talk to people, just removed the infinite scroll behavior. I saved so much time doing so.

Once exams were over, I removed the extension because I allowed myself to doom scroll again. However, the app never really worked the same for me. I would not see relevant news from my friends ever again since then. It's hard to know what happened. Maybe Facebook detected it and decided to punish me. Maybe the entire "algorithm" changed during the time I was preparing and having my exams. Maybe people moved on from Facebook to Instagram and other platforms. Maybe I broke their engagement metrics with the extension and they believed I was only interested in ads and not interested in friends anymore. Maybe they turned on monetization. Hard to tell. From here onwards, it only went downhill.

Around this time, the social media competitive landscape was growing. Picture posting was moving to Instagram. Messaging was moving to WhatsApp. Short form content was moving to Twitter. This was super bad for me. Twitter was really unpopular and Instagram did not allow discussions anymore, just photos and likes. All of a sudden, I no longer had a place for public conversations and blog-like articles. I had to move my writing to medium.com, but no one would find them anymore. Commenting on pictures on Instagram was also a massive downgrade, so most people distilled their insightful conversations to ❤️ heart emojis.

To me, the biggest loss were Facebook groups. The first group I ever joined was my accidentally created classmates group, which evolved to the main place we would share updates for colleagues only. All of this moved to private WhatsApp groups. There was no more discoverability and if you were added to a group, there was complete chaos and continuous notifications.

Then Facebook, the company, moved to greener pastures. They rebranded to Meta and pursued the AR/VR metaverse first, then the AI trend. They invested heavily in Instagram and copied a couple of features, like Snapchat in Stories, the TikTok in Reels and the Twitter in Threads. Facebook, as a product was considered deprecated and bugs started creeping in.

Today, no one is on Facebook anymore, and that, to me, is a very sad state of affairs. Facebook was the original social network. You would follow your friends and you would see their posts along the occasional ad. Instagram, TikTok, X are not **Social** Media. You follow "influencers" that you feel you know, but do not actually know personally. They do not follow you back. They do not interact with you. You **consume** content, but no longer **produce** content, because there is no incentive to do so.

## Scandals

Along the way, there was one major scandal. If you've been reading the news around 2016, Cambridge Analytica and their pink-haired whistleblower should come to mind. If not, let me quickly fill you in.

In 2016, a company called Cambridge Analytica ran a set of campaigns on Facebook to collect user data through a Facebook App called "This Is Your Digital Life". Users would willingly provide data to this app and they would not be informed that it is collected (laughably that is a requirement, but just between us, generally in life, if you give away information, that information is give out forever). Romania taught us this lesson very early on in life. If you share a secret with a friend, the entire school knows it by next day. However, in the West, this came as a surprise when Cambridge Analytica used this data to build psychological profiles on people at scale and then, manged to target ads specifically to those people. This process is widely believed to have influenced the results in the 2016 Donald Trump presidential win and the Brexit vote in the UK. Because of this, Facebook was heavily criticised.

There were a couple of results out of this scandal. The most important one to me is that they removed the Open Graph feature that I used to find friends when visiting new cities. I disliked that, but also understood how that can be used to stalk people and collect data without consent.

A much weirder use case of the data collection and ad targeting issue is the "targeted brainwashing" company that no one seems to talk about, so I will just go into it quickly. I believe that this topic is highly technical, which is why not many people want to get into the weeds and talk about it, but I think it is worth understanding.

Basics: The way ad targeting works is super simple. You visit a site, let's say Apple.com and click on iPhone Pro, then on iPhone Air, then leave the website without buying. Apple just learned that you may be deciding between buying an iPhone Pro and an iPhone Air and you are not interested in (for example) an iPad at all. Through a mechanism called Cookies, Apple can tell Facebook about this and ask Facebook to "help" you decide between the models. Let's say Apple wants you to buy the Pro, they will ask Facebook to always show ads about how amazing the iPhone pro is, never show android ads and don't worry much about iPads because you are not interested, in other words, don't waste effort on that. This is called targeting based on data and it is so effective that, at the moment of writing, Mark Zuckerberg, the CEO of Facebook, is the third richest man in the world.

Now let's say that you want to influence someone's behavior. Let's say you want your wife to consider going specifically to Spain for your next holiday. Let's say that she is open to it, but always considered other priorities, such as Italy and France, so it may be a few years until Spain becomes top of mind, but you really want to go there this year. One thing that you can do is hire this company, and tell them what you want. What they will do is give you a crafted URL that you share with your wife. What the URL does is it registers your wife's deivce via a Cookie, tells Facebook that this "visitor" is super interested in Spain, then redirect the wife to any website you want, say videos of cats. You share the link with your wife, she only sees the final cats, but does not get to learn that a Cookie was "conveniently" placed there for her. Now, every time she goes to Facebook, Google, YouTube and the entire internet, she will start seeing ads about why Spain has the best food in the world, why Spain is better than France and safer than Italy. Those ads can be anything and the company that you just paid, fully controls them. In a few weeks, your wife comes back and says "you know what I want this year? Let's go to Spain".

So I just shared two attacks, one that generated a scandal because it operated at scale, and another one, much more sophisticated and scary, that was not a scandal because it operated on the individual. For some reason, through all of these, I always sided with Facebook because, in my mind, building a tool does not automatically make you responsible for the way people use that tool.

## The Job Market

Moving on from the consumer side of things, Facebook is not just a product. It is a company that employs many Software Engineers like me. And in that regards, it is a very weird and very wild player that wields a lot of power in Silicon Valley by being one of the top two employers in the area, along with Google.

One of the weird things that Zuckerberg does is destabilizing the job market by sending shock waves through it.

In 2022, Facebook decided that it wanted to hire all the talent from the rest of the companies in the USA (don't ask me why). As such, they started throwing money at people, for them to leave their jobs at Apple, Google, Amazon and move to Facebook. I had many colleagues at Apple who would leave my team and join Facebook for literally twice the salary. And it was super disruptive because I would need to either pick up the work that these people left behind them or find a replacement in a company that was being continuously brain drained.

While Apple decided not to fight the brain drain, Google and others decided to counter the poaching by either doing their own poaching or counter-offering the people who were about to leave. This counter-action spiraled the entire market out of control and led to an unprecedented demand for Software Engineers, demand that was seen all the way to the Romanian outsourcing companies that had so much cash thrown at them that they could afford to keep people on the "bench" just waiting for the next project.

However, none of these new engineers were actually needed. The business dynamics did not change significantly and the product usage across the board remained somewhat the same. With the AI hype and hopes for full automation gaining steam, Facebook realized that they did not actually need all of these people who were now sitting around having nothing meaningful to do.

At the end of 2022, Elon Musk bought Twitter, rebranded it to X and fired a large chunk of personnel, including Engineers. This gave Zuckerberg the necessary spark to light up the fire under the entire job market and continued doing one of the biggest layoffs the Tech scene has ever seen, laying off around 11,000 employees, then another 10,000, then another 3,600 - in total about a third of all of their people. All of these people flooded the job market, which led the rest of the industry to follow suit, hire some of these people, but, mostly, have their own layoffs. This was again a huge shockwave that saw people displaced and was felt all the way to the Romanian outsourcing companies that started laying off their own people and gently lowering the real estate market in Cluj Napoca.

In 2025, Facebook once again decided that it is time for fun, just this time in AI. They created a new division called Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL) and started offering crazy salaries, sometimes as high as 100 million dollars (vested over a couple of years) for engineers to join their new division. They were really only targetting the best of the best and people in key positions and, with such high salaries, they managed to get whoever they wanted, but the effect was that the entire industry grinded to a halt. These key people were leading key initiatives at Google, Apple, Amazon, Microsoft, etc and all of a sudden they were gone, destabilizing the entire team and jeopardizing entire projects in the AI space.

That happened this summer. Two days ago, on October 23, Facebook announced that they are laying off 600 AI engineers, completing the clown show and bringing us back to present day.

## The Breakup

Up until 2025, I absolutely loved the company that helped me stay in touch with friends and family, gave me a platform to express my thoughts freely and sponsored many of my University competitions. So what changed?

This year, as I was looking for a change from Apple, I also interviewed with Facebook. To say that it was a terrible experience is an understatement. What stood out to me this time around was that all the managers and engineers whom I had a chance to talk to seemed to be "zombies". 

They all gave me a super negative vibe in a way that is hard to put into words. In a way, they opened my eyes and showed me who are actually the people who run Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Threads, and so on. They made me re-evaluate my long held belief that Facebook is a decent company with decent people and I am ashamed that I was fooled for so long.
