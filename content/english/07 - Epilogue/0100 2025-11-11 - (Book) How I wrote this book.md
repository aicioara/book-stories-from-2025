# How I wrote this book

If you ever wondered what are all the tools and logistics that go into writing a book, or if you wanted to get geeky about publishing, this is the chapter for you. I will break down the exact process that I followed to write the object in your hands.

The beautiful thing about writing is that there is very little that you need to have in order to get started.

### Software for content writing

I am writing all of the content in Markdown files. Markdown is a markup language similar to $Latex$, but simpler. It focuses on content and gets out of your way when it comes to formatting. It has some simple directives such as **bold** or *italic*, headings, titles, tables and images, but otherwise gets out of your way.

For the text editor, I am using Obsidian, a nice piece of software that makes it easier to keep track of references. It formats the text in its final form and runs on both macOS and iOS.

### Hardware for content writing

The majority of the text in this book was written on a MacBook Pro. 

When on-the-go, I prefer to leave my laptop at home, so a minority of these articles are written from my iPad A16 using an external Apple Keyboard that I connect via bluetooth. 

For proofreading, I used my ReMarkable Pro and highlighted / annotated any necessary changes, then manually applied them. This is a tedious process that I do not recommend.

### Images and graphics

The occasional photos were taken with either an iPhone 11 Pro or an iPhone 15 Pro. Some of the karting photos were taken by the event photographer.

Photography stitching was done using the [ImageMagick software suite](https://imagemagick.org/), from the terminal.

Cartoons were all generated by ChatGPT ImageGen 1.

The remaining graphics are screenshots.

### Creating a PDF

Creating a PDF out of Markdown files was more challenging than I expected. I ended up running `pandoc` in a `docker` container with the `lualatex` engine and with the relevant fonts injected into the container.

For a small subset of this book, I had to deviate from Markdown and use $Latex$ to get the look-and-feel that I wanted.

### Publishing

To self-publish the book, I used https://lulu.com. They have many formats and many options available. I ended up settling on A5 page size (148x210mm) (5.8″x8.3″) because, while not popular in the US, it is the most popular format internationally. 

All I had to give lulu.com was the final PDF that I created above, together with a book cover, and they handled the rest.

### Book Cover

For the cover, my initial intention was to use Adobe Illustrator, but midway through writing this book, lulu.com rolled out their own automated cover creator which was surprisingly easy to use and created the final art based on a drag-and-drop editor.

### Distribution

Distribution is still a mystery to me, but I intend to use https://lulu.com to deliver the books as needed.
