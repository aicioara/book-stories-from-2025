# Are we building an AI doomsday machine?

In 1984, James Cameron directed a movie that we were going to remember forever. Robots from the future use a time machine to travel back in time and attack Sarah and John Connor, future leaders of a resistance against a super-intelligent machine called Skynet. The movie was called The Terminator and, nowadays, it is the first thing that comes to mind when we try to imagine the worst case scenario of our current AI trajectory, especially because, in the movie, the robots were sent back from the year 2029, and we are surprisingly close to that date. People tend to do interesting things to make predictions happen.

Today, we understand AI much better than we did in 1984 and we know that Terminator is **not the only** possible doomsday scenario. There are so much more and many more subtle.

*In a diversion from my typical AI posts, today I will write about AI Safety and I will go through a couple of hypothetical scenarios about the future of civilization. These are all made-up, hypothetical situations that may or may not happen, but should help you get an understanding of AI safety and why it is important.*

I will just share what is to me the most likely unsafe scenario to start it off. You know the saying that "you become the average of your closest 5 friends", or that "success breeds success" or that "money comes to money". These are clever variants of saying that people around you have an outsized impact on your life by shaping your beliefs. If everyone around you believes that it is possible to be successful, you will be successful and vice-versa. Today, 800 million people around the earth use ChatGPT weekly and over 1 billion people interact with AI in one form or another. AI assistants crawled into our inner circle and are now part of our "5 closest friends". In fact, I probably chat more with ChatGPT than with my 2nd closest friend. We use (or will use) AI to understand the world and to ask our most pressing questions and we accept the response that comes back the same way we accept the response from any of our friends: with a grain of salt, but ultimately correct. I fear of a world where 9 billion people listen to the same single entity, and where that entity, in its pursuit for greater good, does more harm than expected, totally unintentional. A tragedy of commons. Like recommending an incorrect cure during a pandemic, or recommending a coordinated insurrection.

## Doomsday scenarios

I started the article talking about The Terminator, a story so good and a movie so convincing that most people associate AI doomsday with it. However, while the scenario is still technically possible, I think it is a distraction. Skynet is a barbarian outcome that is meant to generate strong emotions in a movie, but there are other, more subtle ways to achieve a worst-case scenario. Let's talk through some of them.

1. **Group think**. I already shared this as being my most likely scenario because it is the most subtle. We all consume a version of the world that is given to us by AI, and, in our pursuit to building a better place, we destroy it completely. This is very similar to social media that was meant to better connect us, but we ended up in a world where no one lifts their eyes from their phone anymore.
2. **Dictatorship**. The only reason dictatorship regimes of the past could be overthrown was that it was super hard for a dictator to keep the regime functioning at scale over a long period of time. People talk, and plot, and organize, and at some point the boiling pressure is too high and the entire thing explodes. Even if you put microphones in every house, every bar and every person's pocket, it was super hard to keep up with everyone's conversation because you needed people to do the listening and there's only so many people in the government who have time for that. AI can now listen to conversations, label them, cross reference facts to identify intention behind humor and, technically, detect lies. Such a tool in the hands of a single entity with a monopoly over violence is quite scary.
3. **Civil unrest**. If I were to oversimplify Maslow's pyramid of human needs, as soon as the basic necessities of food, water and sleep are satisfied, people need a purpose. Today, purpose for most people is achieved through work (often called career). You go to school, learn a trade and go to work every day, doing whatever you learned to do, whether or not that work is needed. So, what happens when your work is not needed anymore? You'll find yourself with plenty of time in your hands and no purpose, and that is not good. One way to fix this is to purposefully remove some of the basic necessities (water, food, sleep, etc), so that there are too many problems to worry about purpose. That is a scary doomsday scenario. Another is to just ignore it and see what people come up with, but that is scary too. Unfortunately, I don't think we will find a place for purpose-displaced people fast enough to avoid either scenario.
4. **Concentration of power**. Tech companies already have too much power and influence over our day-to-day life. There is nothing you can do if your bank login is slow. There is nothing you can do if Facebook bans you from their platform and all your friends are there. AI research will open avenues that were never possible before. But who should own this?

## Do we really need this?

Have you read Aldous Huxley's Brave New World? You should. I think it's more realistic than George Orwell's 1984. It talks about many things, but the most striking thing for me was his take purpose: from Alphas to Epsilons.

One question that very few people seem to ask is: do we really need this technology and can we accept its consequences. But simpler: do we even need this?

The promise of AI is productivity growth. It started by automating away taxi drivers, continues with warehouse workers, QA engineers, factory workers, paralegals and eventually takes robotic forms to finally go after farmers, cleaners, cooks, plumbers, firefighters and everything that currently requires physical presence.

The promise is that, through automation, we reduce the cost of goods and services for everybody.

If you no longer need to pay for the Amazon delivery worker, or for the warehouse personnel, your online shopping deliveries will be cheaper, maybe even free.

If you no longer need to pay for the waiter and the chef at the restaurant, your cooked food will be much cheaper.

If you no longer need to pay for the fruit picker, and you also happen to own land, all your fruits will be free and you do not even need to go there to pick them or to take care of the field.

But is that really true? Finding the person will **actually** pocket the difference between the old cost and the new cost is not trivial. Will it be cheaper for you? Or will Amazon keep more profits (and distribute them to the shareholders)? Or will NVIDIA and the data centers absorb that value? Hard to tell.

More importantly, what happens to all of us? I already mentioned that people need purpose. You probably already heard about the concept of a BS job. It is a job that exists only to keep you there. If you have 15 minutes, watch Omeleto's master piece on YouTube https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PAK1VcjHD3A.

Why do people take BS jobs? Because people need purpose. Why do BS jobs exist? Because people need purpose. If we really wanted to be more productive as a society, we'd cut away that waste, much like what D.O.G.E. tried to do in the beginning of 2025 with poor success and great backlash.

The way I see it, job automation will go one of a couple of ways. From the happiest to the darkest:
1. AI will never actually be able to fully replace jobs, so we just co-exist and adjust our jobs from doing work to managing (the AI) work and stepping in only when necessary.
2. We create more BS jobs to move displaced people into.
3. We lay off all people and never find a job for them, but we give them some form of Universal Basic Income or some form of pension.
4. We lay off all people and give them shares in the companies that own the robots, making all people investors, not workers. All the productivity value that AI produces does not go to consumers (through lower costs), but goes to the corporations and it is then distributed down to the shareholders. If you are a shareholder, you get dividends, otherwise you are homeless forever.
5. We lay off people and do nothing. People are unemployed and have no savings and there is no social net to help leading to civil unrest.
6. We (or the AI) forcefully reduce the world population, so that it adjusts to the actual demand for labor. It can be done to the active population (through war or engineered pandemics) or the coming population (through birth control or child incentive removals).

But, wasn't option 0 to just stop AI tech development, like the protesters hope? And also, why am I talking about all of this while also working at OpenAI in the same time? Isn't that hypocrisy?

Personally, I don't think anyone can stop technological advancements. If one person does not work on AI, someone else will be sufficiently motivated to do so. If OpenAI does not do it, another company will. If USA does not do it, some other country will. If the entire planet does not do it, some kid in his basement will do it behind everyone's back.

Finally, assuming we can do something about it (again, we can not), should we stop AI development? What benefit does it actually bring us? Computers creating the spreadsheets and the keynote slides instead of us? Did we need those spreadsheets in the first place? Self-driving cars taking us from A to B. Did we need to go to B in the first place?

Maybe a simpler life is a better life. Every technological innovation from the past has disconnected us further from nature: agriculture, trading, industrial, computers and now AI.

However, adding all of those advancements together is what actually separates us from monkeys, which is an interesting thought experiment. Let's for a second imagine that we are living like monkeys. Maybe we have some sort of basic clothing to avoid being cold, or maybe we live in warmer climates, but otherwise lifestyle is identical. Is that a better life or a worse life?

We would be closer to nature, not need to go to work Monday through Friday, not have to pay for food, not be stressed, have meaningful connections with our peers, live in actual communities, not need to pay for rent, most diseases just kill the weakest.

Is that a better life or a worse life? I think each of our answers would end up somewhere along a spectrum, but, in my opinion, life is a lot more fun when you do modern stuff and you have to worry about things, than if you just get your bananas from the tree every day and your biggest issue is that your monkey spouse cheated on you.

## Conclusion

I will leave you with a story. It is actually a well known tale that you may already know, but with so many derivatives of this story out there that it was hard for me to find the original. In the spirit of the AI era, I will create my own derivative of this story, just for you.

> A Software Engineer wakes up in the morning, pours herself a cup of coffee, logs in to her computer and changes a server config. The server fleet takes the config and scales up to more computers to handle the Black Friday traffic that is expected that day. The engineer finishes her coffee and closes the computer. She is done for the day.
>
> The next day, same story, similar config change. The server is now scaling down, no more Black Friday. The engineer is done for the day and goes to the park
>
> A businessman sees the woman in a park one afternoon and asks.
>
> - \[Businessman\] I am sorry to interrupt your walk. I can't help but notice that you are here, walking in the park every day, without any worry in this world. If you do not mind me asking. What do you do for a living?
> - \[Engineer\] I am a Software Engineer.
> - \[B\] Oh, that must be a lot of money, right? Are you early retired?
> - \[E\] Nope, and it's actually not a lot of money for this particular job. But I do like the job. I get to make sure that the company can handle all users that want to buy items here. I automated most of my work, so I just need to press one button in the morning now.
> - \[B\] Is your boss okay with that?
> - \[E\] Yes, they are super happy that the website is working all the time and that they do not have to pay me much for that service.
> - \[B\] So that takes you one minute a day. What do you do with the rest of the time?
> - \[E\] **I walk in the park, then do some yoga, spend time with my children, read on the beach, go out with the girls, and make love with my husband.**
> - \[B\] You seem like a really talented young woman. If you wanted to, with my business skills, and your automation skills, we could do wonders.
> - \[E\] How so?
> - \[B\] Well, first, I can probably find you a couple of clients that need a similar service and are willing to pay a similar price. We could create a company and deliver this 1-minute a day button to each of them.
> - \[E\] And then what, sir?
> - \[B\] We could live on the salary of just one clients, like we do now and reinvest the money into the business. We would create even better automation, so that the task can be batched for all clients and reduce the overall time
> - \[E\] And then what, sir?
> - \[B\] We could take the extra profit and reinvest it again into the business. We would hire a team of people, so that we can take even more clients and have employees who handle the buttons for all of them.
> - \[E\] And then what, sir?
> - \[B\] We would expand our business worldwide. We would have support staff to answer questions and forward deployed engineers to understand the types of buttons that clients need
> - \[E\] And then what, sir?
> - \[B\] We would start a marketing campaign, and fill the highway with billboards, so that we are on top of every company's mind and we crush all competition. We would be the only button pressing company in the world.
> - \[E\] And then what, sir?
> - \[B\] We would then raise a creative round of funding, where we show our cash flow, sell part of the company to private equity and use the injected capital as colateral for a leveraged loan and use the capital to invest into companies that will then grow and need more buttons, which we can service for additional cashflow
> - \[E\] And then what, sir?
> - \[B\] Then you sell your equity ownership to the private equity for a couple million dollars and you can retire.
> - \[E\] And then what, sir?
> - \[B\] **Then you walk in the park, do some yoga, spend time with your children, read on the beach, go out with the girls, and make love with your husband.**
