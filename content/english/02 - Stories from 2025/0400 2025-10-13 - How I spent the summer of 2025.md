# How I spent the summer of 2025

`June 16, 2025` - `September 30, 2025`

For me, the year starts approximately on October 1 and ends approximately on September 30. My first day of school was September 15, 2000. First day of university was October 1, 2012. First day of work was September 4, 2016.

More importantly, for the past 6 years the Apple Fiscal year started in October 1 and ended on September 30 and is split in 4 quarters. For me, FY25'Q1, FY25'Q2, FY25'Q3, FY25'Q4 (quarters of fiscal year 2025) meant CY2024'Q4, CY2025'Q1, CY2025'Q2, CY2025'Q3 (calendar years).

This article basically covers the end of 2025. I already talked about:
- Q1 - Moving to the US
- Q2+Q3 - How I spent the first 6 months of 2025
- Q4 - This article

With that out of the way, in my previous article, I was just boarding on our plane from Romania and returning to the USA on June 15. Let's continue from there.

Once we returned to the US, we only really had two weeks to chill around. During this time, we attended some summer celebrations at Google and Apple, met with a couple of friends, then, early July, we boarded another plane to Romania.

If this second trip to Romania, only 2 weeks after we spent 1 entire month there sounds unnecessary, you are right, it was, but like last time, it had three objectives.
1. Celebrate Laura's Bday
2. Attend the 24h endurance racing karting competition
3. Attend Alex Stanciu's wedding

For Laura's birthday, we wanted to have a traditional Romanian holiday (in Greece) and we booked an all inclusive resort to just chill on the beach, sleep and read. It was very relaxing and the food was absolutely delicious. We went to Crete, in Heraklion and our hope was to see the beautiful beaches that the island had to offer. We ended up only seeing two of them because there was too much driving and we prioritized relaxing ahead of a very busy period that I will describe soon.

![Chilling in Crete](attachments/IMG_3476.jpg)

Upon returning from Greece, I went straight to Brasov for the 24h race. This was the second of the 3 races in the championship and it was a Le Mans style karting competition. Race would start at 12 noon on Saturday and end at 12 noon on Sunday. Teams had to have a minimum of three people. There were 3.5 of us because Lucian Itu had to depart for his holiday at midnight, so myself, Ionut Banica and Cosmin Pop had to share the drive for the last 12 hours of the race, while Lucian helped on the first 12 hours.

If I remember correctly, Cosmin did the 6 min qualifying and Ionut did the race start, then we settled into a rhythm. Because teams can do pitstops at any time and each pitstop adds about 4 minutes to your time, once pitstops begin, it is very hard to estimate which position you are running in, but we were oscillating between 1st and 4th.

In the afternoon, I got to do my first stint. One thing to know is that the karts are all rentals and we cannot change anything about them: engine, tires, chassis are all provided to us, we just need to drive them. However, karts are not all equal, in fact there are quite large difference between them. One strategy on the race is to follow when a good kart came into pits and got changed (as it is mandatory), then coordinate a pit of your team to grab the better kart. I followed karts for quite a while and when my time came to drive, I picked the best kart on the track and I drove it non-stop for 1 hour and 45 minutes. It was the best time of my karting career.

![Ready for the 24h karting race](attachments/518352888_1167647725401977_388572363635280008_n.jpg)

Since I was the jet-lagged one, it was decided that I would sleep during the day and join them back around midnight, then drive for the team the entire night. I booked an accommodation 5 minutes away from the track and, after watching one more stint, I headed there to sleep.

The race itself is quite uneventful, but there are 3 crucial points: qualifying, starting the race in Le Mans style - you have to run to your kart and then start, and taking the checkered flag. This race was special because they added a twist. At midnight, a safety kart would collect all karts and invert the direction of the race: it started clockwise and it finished anti-clockwise. I was hoping to be part of at least one of the four events, but I was part of none.

I returned to the circuit at 11 PM, hoping that I would get a seat and participate in the direction change at midnight, but Ionut just started a stint and it was crucial that he stays on track because we were running in 4th place and the kart in 3rd place was almost lapping us. If we held position, the safety kart would compact the train and we would effectively gain 45-50 seconds.

At midnight, we were running in 4th position and Lucian left. I was the most reseted and Ionut was almost out of gas, so around 00:30, I was in. If my previous stint was the best of my career, this one was the worst. With the direction of the circuit inverted, I found myself on what was effectively a completely new track. I am not an intuitive driver and I am not a quick learner. My driving skill comes from grinding. By this point, I've done 1,500 km around the track at Prejmer in the clockwise direction, which, at 1.25 km/lap means 1200 laps.

Needless to say, I had and 0 km of experience in the counter-clockwise direction. And karts were passing me left and right. I tried to push a bit harder and, with cold tires I spun twice, barely keeping it on the track and further chipping away at my confidence. If the previous stint was the best of my karting career, this one and everything that followed were by far the worst.

At some point, Cosmin went to sleep, so only Ionut and I were left on the track. Most of it is a blur, but at some point, I got in and picked a really awful kart. Coupled with my poor driving, we were losing 1-2 seconds per lap.

The way we communicate at the circuit is by writing on a small whiteboard with a marker. The driver has some intuition, but generally has no idea how good the times are, so the team in the pits writes down the times on every lap and then writes "PIT" or "BOX" when it's time to change drivers.

For some reason, Ionut decided that, despite this protocol, he would not use the whiteboard and instead signal me to change with his hands only. He was also very cold because he just got out of the kart and was super sweaty, so he changed his t-shirt, jacket, everything. Every lap I would go by the pits, Ionut would signal me to pit to change pilots. But it was 4 AM and super dark outside. Driving at 80 km/h by the pits, I could not recognize his face and his jacket was totally new to me. No one would float the whiteboard, so I just assumed that he went to sleep and left me alone on the track. After 30 minutes I figured out that it could only be Ionut and he was waving me out for a while, so I pitted.

Ionut was absolutely furious because I did not get out to change the kart. I told him that there was no point in me going out and finding him sleeping.

Everything else is a blur, but in the end we finished 4th, with a very sour taste. After celebrations, I headed home and slept from 2 PM until the next morning, then drove home to Alba Iulia.

Returning to Alba Iulia was now exciting and scary. After finishing with the Greece trip and the Karting competition, and until going to Alex' wedding and flying back to the US, I had to put in my resignation from Apple, as I was about to join OpenAI, one week after returning to the USA.

I wrote more about the resignation step in \[How I announced my resignation from Apple\] and I explained the visa constraints more in \[H-1B Executive order\], but I will give a small summary here.

In April 2025, I interviewed with OpenAI, received an offer in May 2025 and accepted it while in my first Romanian trip. Due to a quirk of the offer, joining around the last day of the month had a chance of being financially better than joining around the first day of the following month, mostly due to off by one details. I wanted to join at the end of May, right after my offer, but that was impossible because I had to renew my visa. I then wanted to join end of June, but I did not want to join just to leave for (this) trip to Romania. This meant that the soonest I could join was end of July. More specifically July 28.

On another thread, in the USA, it is customary to give a 2-week notice before leaving your job. Two weeks before July 28 is July 14, which was the Monday I woke up in Brasov, right after my 24 hour race.

On a third thread, changing jobs while abroad is incredibly risky and something that virtually no one does. In summary, for my visa, in order to enter the United States, I need both the visa and an employer. If I were to be fired or laid off, I have 90 days to leave the country unless I find another employer to hire me before the 90 days passed. However, if I left the country earlier than the 90 days, before an employer hired me, I would not be able to enter the United States ever. Giving my notice on July 14, entering the USA on July 21 (as we had tickets) and starting a new job on July 28 would have been fine. But if Apple decided that they actually did not need me (or want to pay me) for another two weeks if I left anyway, I would still be in Romania and would lose both my ability to enter the United States, the new job with OpenAI and Laura's job with Google.

In summary, there were three options right now:
1. Announce my resignation with a 2-week notice and risk both Laura's and my job
2. Announce my resignation with a 1-week notice and jeopardize my relationship with Apple and my coworkers
3. Delay my start date with OpenAI by one week and start in August

Of the three, 2 was a no-go, 3 was a gamble and 1 was just risky.

In the end, I decided with Laura that we'd take option 1 and, by chance, it turned out to be okay. I will talk more about the entire process in a different article.

The last week of Romania was hectic. I celebrated my new job with my parents, then spent a few days in Cluj, attended Alex's wedding, which was really nice, then flew to the USA and had 1 hectic week handing off all of my duties at Apple, then starting my new job at OpenAI.

Since joining OpenAI, life changed in a couple of ways. Due to the increased workload, I had to be more intentional about how I spent my time. The morning and evening commute wasted 2 hours of my day and the traffic dictated my early departure and late arrivals home. I would leave home at 6 AM in the morning to beat traffic and return at 8 PM in the evening when traffic dried down.

Work was incredibly rewarding and very satisfying. Language wise, I changed from Go to Python, which was fantastic. I never liked Go, whereas Python was always my best and favorite language. I dropped the compile time from 10 minutes to virtually nothing and some days I would code for 11 hours straight. Responsibility wise, I went from leading a solo project that no one really cared about to leading a 5-person project that two major releases depended on. Socially, I went from an empty office to a vibrant community of passionate developers.

![Discovering San Francisco](attachments/IMG_4378.jpg)

The most important thing that happened in this transition is that it broke my Instagram and YouTube addiction. No matter what scientists say, these apps are just as addictive as any drug. It is very hard to stop scrolling and I would catch myself opening the apps without even thinking and without even looking at the screen. The brain just knew exactly where on the screen to swipe, despite my attempts to introduce friction and I always scrolled for 10 minutes every time I had to wait for the code to compile.

The stress of the first days at OpenAI, coupled with the disappearance of compile time, coupled with my excitement, made me not even think of Instagram for a few days. Unsurprisingly, that was enough to break the cycle and, while I sound like a former alcohol addict, I haven't touched the app in 2 months and it has been so liberating.

I haven't felt so much freedom since 2011 when, after a 2-week long digital detox summer camp, I quit World of Warcraft, a computer game that I spent 14 hours a day playing.

While Instagram was not as bad as World of Warcraft, quitting it had instant benefits. Most importantly, trying inspired by Ed Sheeran's no smartphone philosophy, I replaced all of that scrolling time with thinking and people-watching. Not only it helped me finally relax, but I started having a lot of great ideas, from articles to write, to architectures to try at work, to fixes back home. I finally gave myself permission to get bored and be creative again, which was incredible.

August and September passed quite quickly. My focus went on a few areas:
- Getting up-to-speed with OpenAI
- Playing and getting better at pool (billiards), as we have a table at work that I use solo
- Writing a book at home
- Spending time with Laura and a couple of our close friends

There is not much relevant stuff I can say about any of them, so I will just leave it there and see you in the next article.
