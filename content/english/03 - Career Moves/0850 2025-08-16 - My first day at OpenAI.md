# My first day at OpenAI

`July 29, 2025`

I am writing this article 3 weeks after the fact. Some things may already be mixed up, but for the most part, I think I recall the majority of the facts. I will try to recall as many small details as possible, in the form of a brain dump and maybe later I can turn it into an article or something.

This is a sequel of my article about \[My last day at Apple\].

---

On the previous night (Monday), at 22:40, I was still at Apple, taking in Apple Park for one last time and taking that epic shot at the reflection pond. I must have gotten home around 23:00, calmed down slightly and jumped right into bed.

Haha! Just kidding. I got home and I realized that I haven't filled in a lot of my workday for OpenAI. Things like I-9, work authorization verification, bank details for pay, etc. I must have gone to bed around midnight or 1 AM.

Tuesday, July 29 2025, 06:00 I am waking up for my first day at OpenAI.

We were strongly encouraged to NOT drive into work, but instead take an Uber. We were given a $75 voucher for Uber, but that would not have covered my commute from Cupertino. We were also told that we will be reimbursed for parking, so I just drove there.

The morning was a bit of a rush, but I got my Massimo Dutti purple polo and white jeans and headed to my car. I was super tired.

Driving to SF was a challenge. I took the 280, but then the GPS told me to merge onto the 380 around SFO Airport and then on 101. On the merge with 380, I almost crashed into someone because of the merge and suddent breaking during the merge. It was super scary and my adrenaline skyrocketed. Since then, I never took that route again.

I got to SF and tried to find the Lot A for the Giants stadium, next to the office. I could not figure out how to go in, so I stopped and asked someone who told me that I have to go all the way round. The entire lot A was super confusing and it took me forever to get there, but I finally parked. I scanned the QR code and paid the $19 for the parking for the day, but for some reason, I could only pay until 5PM. I thought it was going to be enough and I left the car there.

I got to the office around 8:15. We were told that 8:45 is when the introduction starts, but 8:00 - 8:45 is a special breakfast for all new hires. I was nervous about the badge because I submitted my photo for the badge at 1AM the previous night, but, surprisingly, they had it ready for me.

I was super disappointed in the way the badge looked. The one at Apple had an amazing design with our picture and the Apple logo. The OpenAI one was grayscale with no distinct marks.

I badged in and a lady was waiting for me on the other side. She told me that breakfast is on the 2nd floor and showed me a flight of stairs. She asked me if I was okay with taking the stairs, which I found very odd. I confirmed that I am fine with that and made it to the breakfast.

Of course, the breakfast started 15 minutes ago, so I joined a table and met a few folks. I cannot recall any of them, except maybe for Monica, who I found was in HR and was sitting right in front of me.

Breakfast was good and I remembered being impressed that they had smoked salmon and blueberries and that overall it looked much better than Apple's and a bit worse than Google's.

At 08:45, we were ushered into an elevator to the 7th floor for our intro talks.

Because we had to be split across multiple elevators and then had to be taken over the sky bridge from MB2 to MB1, we were waiting upstairs. The area was an open space terrace, much like a rooftop and it was very cold. I was scared for a second that we were going to have our intro there and that I was going to freeze. I did not bring any jacket with me, so that was all the layers I had.

While waiting, a guy showed up and told me that I look like "short Sam Altman". I thanked him and I must have joked something back, like "in fact Sam Altman looks like me". He introduced himself and told me that his name was Andi with an "i", not a "y". I said that my name is Andrei and sometime people also call me Andi (with an "i"). I asked him why he chose Andi as a name (he was Chinese) and he told me that I am correct in knowing that Chinese nationals, when they move to the US, can chose any name. But he was born in Boston, and Andi is his birth name and was chosen like that because it can be easily pronounced in both English and Mandarin.

Andi then told me his story. He was born in Boston, but his family moved back to China for his education. They believed that the Chinese education is much better. There, in the university exam, he scored top 500 and his parents were disappointed, so they moved him to USA to try again and he scored top 26th and went to MIT. Interesting guy.

We made our way to the auditorium and we found 5 rows of seats, 10 across for a class of 50 people that we all started the same day. Andi said we should sit in the middle, but I said no. We should either be last row or first row, but never in the middle of the pack. We ended up sitting in the front row.

The intros were REALLY good. I can recall parts of the first days in a bunch of places
- At Jump Trading, I remember them telling us that they have an approval rate lower than Harvard. Just 0.25%. I called it bullshit because I knew how statistics work, you can just increase your numerator in the fraction by counting unqualified candidates.
- At Apple, I remember them telling us that the way to succeed at Apple is to make friends. In other words, Apple is a social capital intensive corporation. I called it interesting and I dedicated the next couple of years actually doing that

There are a few things that stand out from these intros, but by August 17 when I am writing this, I forgot most of what happened three weeks ago.
- I remember them telling us that having OpenAI on LinkedIn will put a target on our backs and people will be all of a sudden very interested in us. They told us that they have ex-FBI agents and ex-military and if we want help to keep a low profile, they can help us
- I remember one of the most interesting icebreakers that I've ever seen, where a guy was asking yes/no questions and we had to stand up or sit down based on whether we agreed or not with the statement, then he'd select one person from each side to defend their position. All questions were about AI: like will there be a 1-person billion-dollar corporation with the help of AI in the future
- I remember having one of the best handholds ever, where they showed us how to set up EVERYTHING. Super efficient.
- I remember the best codelab, where they showed us how to deploy stuff to production and they told us that OpenAI has a tradition of shipping on day 1. That meant that every team was supposed to have one bug ready for you. You should be able to fix it and merge the code within the next day. Great momentum!

One of the interesting things that happened is that we were gathered in a room for employment verification with HR. I was the only person without a USA passport. I found it so strange. Usually most people in tech are immigrants.

In between these, we had lunch, but I've had so much food at breakfast that the only thing I wanted to have was one peach. I head back to continue setting up my laptop.

I cannot really remember what time we finished with all of these, but eventually I got Slack to work and I texted Andrey, my new mentor. He came up and picked me and Aydan, another guy who started the same day. Andrey then gave us a 1 hour lecture about how the codebase works and what we are working on.

At 5:00, Cristina Scheau, my new manager set up a meeting with me and we chatted about the future. She introduced the Image Search project that I was going to work on and she told me about a bunch of very exciting areas where this is going to play a huge role. She then asked me if I was excited about the project. I sure was!

One of the main issues on this first day were my eyes. First, I haven't had that much sleep. Then, the auditorium room where we spent  most of our day was having a weird strobe light due to some lines in the windows which were causing me issues. Finally, the monitor of my new laptop was, once again, very demanding on my eyes. It turns out that all MacOS laptops M2 and older exhibit this issue. I first saw it on my M2, when I got so dizzy that I accidentally scratched my eye. Then on my M4 MacBook that I could not wait to get rid of. Then on this 14" MacBook Pro. Absolutely terrible.

One of the low lights of my first day was that because I filled in all of my workday paperwork just 12 hours prior, I missed out on two main things: I got a 14" MacBook Pro instead of the 16" model. Because of this, I had to redo my entire setup when I eventually changed to what I wanted. Second, I got the default username, which is `acioara@`, instead of something more custom. This may have been a blessing in disguise because I would have chosen `andrei.cioara@`, to match Apple's email address, which would have caused all sorts of issues due to the period in the name. The best name would have been `andrei@`, but that was already taken by someone who started one year prior. Probably the best name would have been `aic@`, or even `ac@`. Or, if I wanted to get a ton of spam, `ai@` which matches my initials. This handle is used in all systems, but it is the most proeminent in the Slack handle and the email address.

A big shock for me, besides the large 50 people class size, was that everyone was much younger than me. It may be because all new grads started the same day, to align with their school schedule or something. Anyhow, I felt old in a couple of ways:
- Everyone was familiar with Cursor. I was probably the only person there who never used Cursor for coding
- Everyone had a ton of energy. I was visibly tired after one or two 1-hour lectures

However, I also felt weird because everyone also assumed that I was a recent grad and they asked me which university I graduated. I had to tell them all that it was Imperial College London and that it was 10 years ago.

I was super tired, so I headed to dinner and then headed home. As I approached the parking lot, I realized I had no idea where I parked and there must have been about 1000 cars there. It took me 5 minutes to find my car because the key fob is no longer strong enough to reach the car, but I found it in the end and drove back home.

I took Laura and went for a walk around the neighborhood to tell her all about my day. There are a few things that stood out to me then and I haven't captured here yet:
- All people looked super nerdy and anti-social
- All people were American. All people spoke really fast. Even the Asians and Indians whom I met here were born in the USA.
- The security at the place looks off the charts, there are guards patrolling squares around the doors
- Everyone looked super driven and at 7:00 PM, they were still working
