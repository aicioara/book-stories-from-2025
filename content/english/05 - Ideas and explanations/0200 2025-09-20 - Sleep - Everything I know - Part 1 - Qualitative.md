# Everything I know about sleep

I spend 8 hours, every day, either sleeping or attempting to sleep. Because the day only has 24 hours, that means that I am spending a third of my life sleeping.

This aspect dawned on me 15 years ago and since then, I not only prioritized sleep above everything else, but I actively tried to improve it. I read a lot about sleep and I experimented a lot to see what works and what doesn't.

What is interesting about sleep books is that, in order to sell, their author needs to have a "background". Most often, they are doctors of some sort, that spent their entire career studying sleep and treating insomniacs. The books are intended for large audiences, but sleep is a deeply personal topic. Writing a book about sleep is like writing a book about what girl or what boy you should marry. You cannot do that because we are all different.

So why write about sleep? Because we are all different. This is my account about how to get better sleep. With that said, I am not a doctor, I am a Software Engineer that spent the last 15 years experimenting with all sorts of tricks to get better sleep. Today, I feel like my sleep is 2-3 times better than 15 years ago. I want to document what I know today, first for my future reference and second for you. I am 100% sure that you will find that some of my tricks will help you greatly because you and I may be similar in some regard.

While I have both qualitative and quantitative data, in this post, I will only focus on the qualitative aspect, so I do not get you bored with graphs, but watch out for a follow-up, where we will geek out on 6 years of sleep data that I measured using Apple Watch consistently.

In this article, I will talk about all the tools, tricks and routines that I added over time to get my sleep in the perfect spot. I will also touch on a couple of things that did not work for me, but worked for many others and may work for you. I also discuss both my setup at 1. USA home, 2. My Romanian home and 3. On the go. Finally, I will leave you with some references and further reading. Let's dive right in.

## Equipment

There are people who like to buy things and there are people who like to change habits. Personally, I feel like buying things is much easier than changing habits, so I will start with that. These are all the items that I currently use to sleep. I spent countless hours on researching, buying and sometimes throwing each one of them. This is my advice when you buy or upgrade your own.

#### Mattress

The mattress is by far the most important contributor to sleep quality. USA businessmen recognized that fact and created entire chains such as [Mattress Firm](https://www.mattressfirm.com/), that specialize in selling you overpriced mattresses. I spent countless hours in such stores, trying out mattresses and talking to the managers and their staff, to the point where they were throwing discounts at me just to leave them alone.

Mattresses come in a couple of sizes, that are different between continents. In Europe, height is usually 200 cm and widths are 90 (single), 140 (small double), 160 (regular double), 180 (large double) and 200 (very large double). 90 cannot fit 2 people, 140 barely fits 2 people, 160 is the most common one, 180 is considered luxury, 200 is very rare, but my favorite.

In the USA, the sizes are Single, Twin, TwinXL, Full, FullXL, Queen, King. Single, Twin and Full have the same height. Despite its name, Twin can actually only fit one person, but it is called like that because two twins are as wide as a King, so many couples (and hotels) buy a King bed frame and add two Twin mattresses inside. Full is a bit wider, but generally meant for one person. The XL versions of Twin and Full are just longer, so that the height is now aligned with the Queen and King. In fact, I lied earlier, hotels buy 2x Twin**XL** mattresses that have the same surface as a King, but are now separate. So Single, Twin, Full have same height. TwinXL, FullXL, Queen and King also have the same height and different widths, very similar to Europe now: Twin is for one person, Full is for one person who is afraid of falling out of bed, Queen is for two people on a budget and King is a luxury for two people. Hotels/Motels in the USA come with two options: either 2x queen beds, intended as one bed per individual (or one bed per couple on a budget) or 1x king bed, intended for couples. Personally, I can comfortably share a Queen or a King and uncomfortably share a Full.

Mattresses also come in levels of firmness: soft, medium and firm. This is a personal preference, but lighter people and side sleepers should prefer softer, while back and stomach sleepers and heavier sleepers should prefer firmer. For my weight and my side sleeping, a firm mattress feels like a piece of wood: it does not deform at all and all the pressure is on my waist and shoulders.

For a very long time, I slept on a generic Queen size extra soft mattress from Sleepys that costed $600 (in 2019) and it was fine. The biggest improvement (and investment) for my sleep was Purple. At the beginning of this year, I spent $1,600 (+tax) on a TwinXL Restore® Hybrid Mattress from Purple.

I knew about Purple from back in 2019, when I tested their mattress and I was mesmerized. It was much better than ANYTHING I've ever tried, but back then it was $5,000 for a new and $3,000 for a used one. I did not want to take a used mattress and $5,000 was a big pill to swallow for an unproven technology.

Prices did not necessarily fall in 2025, but they opened up their offerings. They introduced sizes and categories: Essential® Collection, Restore® Collection and Rejuvenate® Collection. Rejuvenate® was way too expensive and they had too many bells and whistles. Essential® was great, but all the mattresses were firm, and I also could not try them in any store. Restore® was basically the best mattress money can buy that offers direct contact with the lattice technology (more on this in a bit). I also realized that I can do with a smaller size. I could get a TwinXL and place it alongside the existing Queen size bed, so I could practically just extend the width of my existing bed, without changing much otherwise. This gives the best of all worlds: keeps cost small, I can have the firmness that I want, independent of my wife, and we still have a Queen size bed.

What is special about the Purple mattresses is a solid gel-like lattice that exists on top of the mattress. Very similar to a mattress topper (sometimes called a "plush" that is used to soften the mattress). The lattice is specially designed for side sleepers and it creates the perfect pressure around shoulders and waist.

I used to wake up every day with lower back pain and I just assumed that it is a normal thing that happens as I age past 30. Since I upgraded to the Purple, I stopped having that. Also, it is much easier for me to fall asleep because the mattress is both firm and soft at the same time, so it gives me a feeling of "home", while not being saggy. At least this was the best I could do to describe the feeling in words, it's really one of those things that you need to try to understand.

At $1,600, the Restore® Hybrid Mattress in TwinXL size, was, by far, the best purchase of 2025. I am only sad that I cannot send a couple to Europe because they only deliver in the USA.

#### Sheets

Bedsheets usually mean pillowcases, blanket cover, mattress fitted sheet. Some people use flat sheets and mattress protectors, but I do not. Some people use blankets without a cover, I do not, personal preference.

Sheets come in a couple of material variations, but the most common in the USA are: poliester, cotton, sateen, modal and linen.

I had my fair share of poliester as a poor student, but, generally speaking, poliester should be avoided at all costs. The only reason it exists is because it is much cheaper. The biggest issue is that it cannot be washed at 90º and it creates static charges as you move around the bed. The static charges make everything stick to you and the low electrical field that it creates leads to infertility (same for polyester underwear). It also feels harsh to the skin, so all in all, not cozy at all. The other four materials are fair game.

Cotton is the most common and it is the one used in hotels because it can be washed at 90ºC, killing most bacteria. It comes in thickness levels, the thicker it is, the more expensive, the more premium it feels. Cotton also comes in two additional flavors: Organic Cotton and Egyptian Cotton. I don't know if those help besides fluff marketing (again, thread count is the most important metric).

Sateen, from what I understand, is also cotton, but treated in some way. It feels very much like silk, but it can be washed and it is generally considered more premium. The main advantage of sateen is that it has very little friction, so it always flows off of you, just like silk. For people with longer hair, this helps with hair loss because it does not pull on your hair as much. It is also preferred by people who like to feel cool/cold during sleep. Because the material barely touches you, the bed feels very cold. When I first bought a sateen fitted sheet and blanket cover, I hated it because it felt so impersonal. There was nothing hugging you anymore, it feels like the bed stays as far away from you as possible.

Modal is a syntetic extract that is made out of wood. It became very popular for underwear and recently transitioned to bedsheets. It is considered the most premium material because it is the softest. It is also the polar opposite of Sateen. The material really hugs you and feels very warm and cosy, but if you prefer feeling cold during sleep, avoid this.

Linen is now seen as a more natural alternative, but also a niche material which I never tried.

My choice?
- I use a fitted sheet and pillow cases of 100% cotton, with the highest thread count (thickness) I could find (800). I got them from Target for about $60. I wash them at the highest temperature and they look amazing all the time.
- I use a modal blanket cover to keep me warmer and feel cozyer. I got it from Calvin Klein off Amazon for $40.
- In Romania, I also use a modal fitted bedsheet, also from Calvin Klein, also for $40, but it got out of shape quite quickly, which is why I decided against it in my new setup.

#### Blanket

Blankets differ in their filling (natural down or synthetic), size and thickness. Personally, the blanket does not matter much to me. I just got the cheapest one from Traget, for $10 during some back-to-school sale and I wrapped it in the Calvin Klein modal sheet. The most important thing here is that the sheet matches the blanket. The most important feature of the blanket is keeping you warm. Some people use a fitted sheet to cover during the summer, but I find that to be too cold. I keep the same blanket all year round, hot or cold and, if needed, I regulate temperature by leaving one leg exposed, rather than ditching the entire blanket.

One thing to know here is that there is a product called a "heavy blanket". They are literally designed to push you down into the bed and are very calming to people with anxiety or autism. I've unfortunately never tried it because they are expensive and a pain to move around, but it would be one of my next experiments.

#### Bed frame

For the bed frame, the only important thing is that it is silent and allows the mattress to ventilate. I got the second cheapest ($50) foldable bed frame from Home Depot and I just placed the mattress on top.

For quality, go for a well known tool store: Home Depot / Dedeman, as they have higher quality items than mattress stores or generic supermarkets.

Raising head is nice while reading, rising feet is just a gimmick and the vibration/massage is something I never really use. But it was a nice experience.

This is one item I really do not care about. I usually sleep in my underwear and an oversized cotton t-shirt. The t-shirt is dedicated just to sleeping, and I never wear it anywhere else.

## Extra purchases

Besides the basics, here are some extras items and things to think about, that you really do not need, but make sleep and rest so much better and more effective.

#### White noise

I first heard about the concept of White Noise when I visited a friend in Cambridge. Someone in the group kept saying how much sleeping with white noise changed their life and how much better they slept now. I found the entire concept so weird that I remembered it, even now 10 years later.

White noise is a constant sound that is neutral and incorporates many frequencies. It is very similar to the sound of the wind or the sound of a waterfall. The idea behind it is that it masks most other sounds. You know how you cannot really hear other people at a concert or in a car with the windows open? Same idea, white noise drowns other sounds.

The crazy thing, and why this is helpful, is that our brains are very good at filtering things out. Especially things that are constant, so even though there is this sound that drowns everything around, the brain filters it out and the result is somehow perfect silence. If you never experienced that, you need to try it to believe it.

Note here that some people get very tired from brain's passive work of filtering out sound, but I am not one of those. 

White noise has a positive effect on me. It helps me is by reducing sleep interruptions. If a dog barks, or a door closes, or a car revs up the engine, I cannot hear those things at all.

For the white noise, I have a couple of tools.

In Romania, I use a $100 Philips air purifier set on maximum speed. This creates a wind sound and has the added benefit of removing all air pollution from the room.

In the USA, I have a 10 hour white noise mp3, very similar to https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nMfPqeZjc2c, that I stream on a cheap $20 JBL GO 2 bluetooth speaker, that is always connected to power. The reason I stream on external speaker and not on the laptop speakers is because I believe the white noise will ruin the speaker, so I am using a throwaway speaker that is much cheaper to replace.

On the go, I use a product called [Pocket Sleep 1 from DreamEgg](https://dreamegg.com/products/pocket-sleep-1). It is basically a bluetooth speaker without the bluetooth part, it has a set of 16 white noise loops stored on its internal memory, so all I need to do is turn it on. The battery lasts for an entire night (but can also be plugged in) and it is half the size of your closed fist (or the size of a smaller chicken egg), making it super easy to carry around on holiday.

The trick here is to get really used to one sound and not change it. This is why I have the white noise mp3 local on my laptop and backed up in 3 places, so I never lose it.

#### Earplugs

I do not use earplugs at home at all. My place is quiet enough and the whitenoise fixes all issues. My ears also start hurting after 2-3 consecutive nights of using them.

However, I always travel with earplugs and they were a mandatory accessory when living with roommates. The trick here is to buy the most expensive ones, not the cheap throw-away ones.

I personally use the [Hearos Ultimate Softness Original Formula](https://www.hearos.com/collections/sleep-1/products/ultimate-softness-series-ear-plugs). They are rated for 32 DB noise reduction (in some frequencies). With them, I cannot hear high frequencies anymore. Paired with white noise for the low frequencies and I cannot hear anything anymore.

Care must be taken because you can easily get ear infections. To avoid that, I use an airtight [Portable Ear Plugs Containers](https://www.amazon.com/Portable-Carrying-Urekt-Waterproof-Container/dp/B0CL5WGBKY) from Amazon. I take 3-4 of those with me on a holiday and they are enough to last the entire time.

#### Timekeeping and alarms

I will soon go into my sleep routines, but one important thing for me is being able to tell time. I unfortunately wake up 1-2 times a night and I need to know how much longer I can sleep. I also need something to tell me when to wake up.

In Romania, I have a digital nightstand watch that shows the hour in red color, so it does not disrupt the sleep. In the USA, I use my Apple Watch both for telling time and waking up. On travel, I use the Apple Watch as a flashlight to find my way to and from bed.

If I need an alarm to wake up (and I usually don't), I prefer to use the Apple Watch as well. I noticed that being woken up by haptic feedback on the wrist is much smoother than waking up using sound (like an alarm). And to me, smooth waking up is the most important thing in starting my morning. Also, with all the white noise, it is sometime hard to hear the alarm.

When I have something important the next day, like a plane or something not-to-be-missed, I generally a haptic alarm and multiple audio alarms as backups.

#### Light bulbs

The light bulbs that I use while I am awake are super important to my sleep. Back in the day, we only had incandescent light bulbs throughout the house and maybe some neons in the kitchen and bathrooms. These days, those technologies are gone, and in some countries outright banned, and the only option that we have are LED lights. Side note before moving on: incandescent lights are the best for your eyes and sleep.

The thing to know about LED is that it generally comes in 5 colors: red, green, blue, warm white and cool white. Red, green and blue can be combined to give any color, but are typically not used for your house, so we are looking at warm white and cool white. Sometime you will also see "neutral" white, but that is really just a blend of warm and cool. Some producers cheap out when building the neutral light, so what they do is that they flicker between warm and cool, creating a strobe effect. Because of that, unless you know your producer, avoid neutral white.

So the only two options that you have are warm white (rated at 2700K) and cool light, sometimes called daylight white (rated at 6500K). One thing that is specific to our eyes is that blue light suppresses melatonin production, and melatonin is the hormone that triggers sleep. For that reason, it is advisable to avoid screens (TV, laptops, phones), blue lights, and **cool white lights** before sleep.

The thing to remember is that warm white encourages sleep, while daylight white makes the sleep go away. It is best to have warm white in the evening and cool white in the morning, but if you cannot do that, then just have warm white all the time, like I do.

#### Night lights

If you must visit the bathroom during the night, avoid white light at all costs. What I do is I have red LED lights plugged in outlets around the house. They turn on automatically when the light is low, so I don't need to think about them, and they draw very little current.

#### Full blackout blinds

By window blinds, I mean the exterior blinds that one can install to create total darkness in the room. They can be paired with total blackout curtains on the inside.

I use those in Romania when I am trying to keep a California timezone for work. They are wonderful because your brain cannot tell what time it is anymore, so it does not wake up at 7 AM when you mean to wake up at 10 AM (more about this on my sleep schedules).

One added benefit is that you can program them to gently open automatically around the time you plan to wake up, adding one extra dimension to the alarms that you may have.

In the USA, I do not use those because they are expensive and I wake up before sunrise anyway.

#### Water

Finally, I keep a bottle of water next to my bed all the time. I try to not drink from it during the night, but if needed, it's better to have it there than to search for it and wake up fully in the process.

## Habits and Guidelines

Alright, so buying was the easy part. Throw some money at the problem and part of it goes away. You don't really need anything other than money to reproduce my exact setup or to pick up parts of it. In this section, I want to talk about the habits that improved my sleep just as much as all of the equipment and setup. They require no money, but they require a ton of discipline.

#### Sleep tracking

In general, I strongly believe that you cannot change what you cannot measure. It is very easy to measure sleep purely based on how well you feel the next day, what is your performance, and how easy you wake up.

With that said, there are sleep tracking devices and I personally used the Apple Watch to track my sleep for the past 5 years and the iPhone to log my sleep for the psat 6 years.

There are also other devices, such as Oura Ring, Android Watches and Garmin Watches that do similar tracking and also give you a "personal battery" score for how well you should feel.

One thing to say here is that there is very little you can do with that data, so do not get too hung up on it. You don't really need your Oura to tell you that you are at 50% battery when you wake up. You can already feel it. Likewise, there is very little you can do despite knowing how much time you spent in Deep Sleep vs REM Sleep vs Core Sleep. It is somewhat useful to know how many times you woke up during the night and how long you actually slept (not just how much time you spent in bed), but for the most part you cannot do much with that information, besides its entertainment value of seeing it for the first time.

However, one really interesting thing about sleep tracking is that you can absolutely see correlations with the habits that you are trying to build. In other words, if you don't change anything, there is no much you can do with knowing how well you slept, but if you are trying to change something, you can actually see improvements in the data.

#### Bedtime and wake up time

If you only take one thing out of this article, remember that the bed time, wake up time and the mattress that you use are the three most important factors to your sleep.

(I will dedicate an entire section to the specific examples of bed times and wake up times that I experimented with for the past 15 years.)

I strongly believe that your day starts the moment you go to bed. Everything about the next day was already decided the moment your head touched the pillow in the evening.

The key thing here is consistency. You should think long and hard what works for you, pick a sleep time and a wake up time, and stick to them.

It will be hard. Really hard. And it will require sacrifices.

These days, I go to bed between 9:30 and 10:00 and I wake up between 5:30 and 6:00, totalling 8 hours of sleep. The sacrifice that I am making is nightlife and social events. Most events start in the evening, generally 7 through 8 and rarely finish by 9. I generally decline all such events, or try to propose earlier times. That is not always possible, which leads to a horrible following week, trying to adjust to the schedule again. I can also see it in the data.

What I gain by waking up at 6 is that there is no rush to get to work, so I can take my time and still beat traffic.

Another sacrifice that I am making is the week-end. The schedule must stay the same during both week-day and week-end, otherwise I will need the first 2-3 days of the week to adjust to the new schedule after a Saturday sleep in. Again, I can see that in the data.

Conclusion: the time you go to bed and wake up is not very important, as long as it is consistent. This requires sacrifices.

#### Alcohol

Alcohol in the evening has a negative effect on sleep. I can also see it in the data. How much it affects depends from person to person. I tend to not see a difference between 0 and 1 beer at 5% alcohol and 330 ml, but I can definitely notice change in the data when going from 1 beer to 2 and beyond, or even at a single 500 ml bottle.

Because of that, I try to limit alcohol as much as possible and generally stay under 1 beer.

#### Coffee and energy drinks

I do not drink coffee and I do not drink energy drinks (Red Bull, Monster, etc). I have the occasional sugar drink (Pepsi, Fanta) once or twice a year. Because of that, I cannot comment much on the effect of these drinks on sleep.

The reason I do not drink coffee is that I want to be more in tune with my body. I want to feel sleep when I should be sleepy and address the root cause, rather than the symptom.

#### Food

Digesting food while sleeping negatively affects sleep. Because of that, I have my last meal of the day around 6:00 PM, so that I can be ready for sleep around 9:30 - 10:00 PM. This requires sacrifices.

In general, I do not make as many sacrifices with food as I do with bedtime and wake up time. However, there was a time when I had a strict 6-hour rule: do not eat 6 hours before bed. In practice, that meant dinner at 5 PM and sleep at 11 PM. After getting used to being slightly hungry when going to bed, my sleep quality improved a lot.

These days, with the 10 PM bed time, eating at 4 PM, or even 5 PM is very complicated, but if I could improve one thing with my current routine, it would be eating earlier.

#### Water

I limit my water intake a few hours before bed. That results in low-to-no bathroom trips during the night.

#### Meditation

When I was writing my Master's Thesis in University, I was working from the moment I woke up to the moment I went to bed. Every moment I was awake, I would work on my Master's Thesis' research & development. Because of this, I found it very hard to disconnect at the end of the day and fall asleep. I would turn off the light, lay in bed and keep thinking about the experiments I had to perform the following day and the chapters that I had to write.

One thing that really helped me was meditation. A 10 minute meditation session, just before sleep, did wonders to curing my insomnia, so that I could be rested the following day.

For the meditation, I used the Headspace app, which was amazing, and pretty much the only available option in 2016. These days there are many more choices, but Headspace is still a solid first pick and really good for beginners.

I was so grateful on the app that I kept meditating every single day for 1,000 days (which is actually only a bit over three years), but then I stopped and I have yet to restart that.

#### Medication

I have not experimented with sleep-related drugs or supplements, such as melatonin and I do not intend to.

#### Gym

One thing that I noticed is that going to the Gym after work (and before bed) has a negative impact on my sleep. My heart rate tends to stay high after workouts and, as a result, I am having a harder time falling asleep. For sleep, gym is best in the morning.

#### Breakfast

My favorite way to start the day is having a large breakfast. I used to follow a strict "have some protein within the first 30 minutes of waking up" rule, but these days, it is a bit harder, so I am having it within the first 2 hours of waking up.

The glucoze spike that I get after eating breakfast is what actually wakes me up.

#### Morning walk

I discovered that morning walks do wonders for my overall energy in the morning. I am not sure if it is the cold, fresh air or the morning sun hitting my eyes, but it really helps.

#### Snoring

For me, the solution to snoring is to sleep on the side. If I were to sleep on my back, I would wake myself up with my own snoring.

#### Waking Up

The way we wake up dictates how easily we wake up. I imagine everyone is different, but this is my favorite order:
1. No alarm
2. Visual Alarm (a lamp that gradually turns on when it's time to wake up)
3. Haptic Alarm (Apple Watch or any smart watch)
4. Audio Alarm (Phone alarm)
5. Having someone wake me up

Let's unpack these.

Having someone wake me up is the worst. It makes me uncomfortable and it reminds me of the times I could not wake up in middle school. I pretty much never do this.

Audio alarms are the norm, but I got to hate the sounds of the alarms pretty quickly. These days, audio is only a backup for the haptic.

Haptic alarm is what I use 99% of the time. Basically, I set up the alarm in the evening on my iPhone and my Apple Watch vibrates in the morning to wake me up. This helps me a ton by boosting my energy.

Visual alarms are a luxury. It means that the room is dark enough that you actually notice a change in light intensity. It works in my own apartment in Romania, where I created the perfect conditions, but in the rentals in the USA, I can pretty much never do that. If I could though, this is the nicest and most natural way to wake up.

No alarm is the ultimate luxury. It means that the sleep schedule is so good that I wake up at the correct time every morning. I can somewhat get this to work by setting up a backup alarm at 6AM and deciding that any wake up between 5AM and 6AM is the final wake up and I should get up. Once you get used to it, this works quite reliably and when I hit my 6AM alarm, that's a sign that I went to bed too late the previous night.

#### Temperature

I personally dislike behing too cold and I can always sleep in any form of heat. I believe I am the exception, not the norm.

However, one thing that I noticed is that lowering the temperature of the room gives me better sleep, so I am trying to have my body get used to lower temperatures while sleeping.

An easy way to reduce temperature is to turn on the AC 15-30 minutes before bed time and then sleep with the window open and the AC off. In California, the temperature at night is in the low teens ºC, so the room never actually heats up from where the AC left it at the beginning of the night, staying in the high teens ºC throughout the night.

Another interesting fact is that falling asleep is easier on lower temperatures, so that is a bonus.

#### Screen usage

One of the most common things that you will read online is that screen time right before bed time affects sleep. Screen time means TV, computers, phones, etc. Basically any device that can produce blue light or can reproduce any color.

The science behind this is that humans evolved to only expect blue light (or cool light) during daytime. For centuries, the only light during the night would be fire based, and that was always colored warm yellow or red. Blue LEDs are a very recent invention and the human brain still associates it with the daylight. As a result, the natural melatonin production is disrupted and it is harder to fall asleep.

Personally, I found that **not** to be the case. Maybe I spend so much time in front of devices, that I became immune to it, but I realized quite the opposite. In my experiments of not touching the phone 1 hour before sleep, I found out that it is actually harder for me to fall asleep. Mostly because dropping the phone/laptop means less distractions, so my brain recovers and I get a lot of energy, making it harder to fall asleep, whereas jumping from work straight to sleep (assuming I can calm my mind with meditation) is much better.

#### Sleep position

I personally find that sleeping half-way between side and stomach is the best.

- If I sleep on my back, my throat collapses and I wake up gasping for air.
- If I sleep on my stomach, my face has nowhere to go. If I keep my face to the side, my neck hurts. If I use something like a massage chair, my sinuses get blocked.
- If I sleep on my side, it is generally fine, but as I aged, I found it harder to do so because my hips and shoulders start hurting after a couple of hours of sleep.
- Half way between side and back is great, but I often fall towards my back and I wake up
- Half way between side and belly is the ideal position. Basically start on the side, then lean slightly towards a belly position until the pressure is off the hips and shoulders.

I can achieve this position in three different ways:
- Spooning with my wife
- Hugging a pillow
- Having a 2-person blanket for myself and hugging the unused part

#### Sleeping in

I try not to sleep in on the week-end. Ever. That is because it messes with my sleep schedule, so I will spend the Monday, Tuesday and sometimes Wednesday trying to readjust to the proper sleep schedule.

With that said, about once every few months or weeks I sleep in when my body requires it. More often than not, that's a rainy day, because weather really affects my energy levels. The way I usually do it is waking up at the usual time, going to the bathroom, and if I still feel sleepy, I decide that I need 2-3 more hours, set an alarm and get back to bed.

#### Naps

I love napping, but I rarely nap. However, I do take one nap every couple of months / weeks, when I feel very tired, very similar to sleeping in.

One trick that I found is that limiting the sleep time with an alarm to a total of 35-45 minutes achieves amazing results. This is sometimes called a power nap and the idea is that I need 5 minutes to fall asleep and then 30 minutes to stop feeling sleepy. Anything longer than that would affect my bedtime, but 30 minutes is the perfect time for a quick recharge without affecting my sleep routine.

#### Hygiene

Something very important to me is hygiene. I think these are items common sense for most people, so, just quickly sharing them:
- I make my bed every morning, so the room looks tidy
- I was my bedsheets once or twice a month
- I brush my teeth every time before going to bed
- I have a shower right after waking up

## My Latest Tricks

This year (2025), I started experimenting with sleep again and I found a couple of new tricks that I did not know before. To me, these were the biggest improvements to what I think was already a pretty good sleep hygiene.

The goals that I have in mind are three fold:
- Make it easy to fall and stay asleep
- Wake up energized and excited
- Have energy throughout the entire day, until it is time to sleep again.

#### Night time books - Falling asleep

Reading a book right before bed did wonders to my ability to fall asleep. One thing that it achieves is that it takes my mind away from the problems I had to solve at work, so that I can fall asleep.

More importantly, and this is hard to explain, it makes my eyes tired and dry. For some reason, my brain associates dry eyes with time to sleep and I sometimes fall asleep with the Kindle in my hand.

It is important to mention that I read on my Kindle, or, worst case scenario, from a paper book, but never from a LED device, such as a phone or tablet.

#### Stretching - Staying asleep

One thing that I added to my evening routine is stretching. Nothing super fancy, just one or two minutes where I stretch my legs and, more importantly, my glutes.

I believe there are some stress relief hormones that get released during stretching and those have a side effect of promoting sleep. I don't think it necessarily helps me fall asleep as much as it helps me stay asleep and have better quality sleep.

#### Cooling up the room - Falling asleep

I might have already mentioned this, but a neat trick that I have since I got an AC was to cool down the room 15-30 minutes before going to bed. I did not actually measure the final temperature, but I'd say it's 18-19ºC by the time I turn off the AC and get into bed.

The fact that the bed is cool makes it softer to the touch, which, together with the Purple Mattress and the Calvin Klein blanket cover makes everything really cozy for me and helps me fall asleep faster.

#### Open window - Waking up energized

One night this year, I forgot the window open and went to bed. I woke up in the morning extremely energized. So much, in fact, that I was puzzled.

I realized quickly the correlation between the open window and my energy levels in the morning. I tried closing it some nights and leaving it open other nights and the correlation was almost perfect.

I am lucky enough to live in the California suburbs, so I have no traffic, no dogs, no birds and no noise during the night. Coupled with the white noise that I mentioned earlier, having the window open is unnoticeable.

I must add that I tested this between July and October, so it remains to be seen how well can I reproduce this during the winter days.

What I believe happens here is that the open window maintains a low CO2 concentration in the room. I invested in a CO2 sensor a couple of years ago and I was shocked to see just how quickly it increases from 400 PPM to 1000 PPM or even 2000 PPM in a single 1-hour Zoom meeting.

Sleeping for 8 hours with the door closed has a similar effect. I knew about this for a while and I used to sleep with the bedroom door open, but sleeping with the bedroom window open is just so much more effective.

#### Own a bed - Energy throughout the day

This was a big one. Controversial, yet intuitive.

Sharing a bed with your partner is an expectation. It is a cultural norm that has many benefits, mostly increased intimacy. However, it is somewhat strange that we share the bed only to dimiss each other for 8 hours while both asleep.

The biggest issue with sharing a bed is that any movement of one partner wakes up the other and vice-versa, leading to fragmented sleep.

Having my own bed made a lot of intuitive sense, but I definitely did not want to lose the intimacy, so this took a lot of time and thinking to get right.

I started by stopping sharing the blanket. This had an immediate positive impact, as things would move less often, yet we still shared the bed.

The next improvement was buying a secondary, smaller bed and place it next to the main bed. This way, we would fall asleep in the main bed and I would roll over to the seconday bed at some point during the night.

We have evolved that further and we now moved the small bed in a separate bedroom that I use during the week and I return to the shared bed during the week-end.

I think that is a decent compromise and sacrifice for better quality sleep, and I am incredibly grateful to Laura for working with me through this experiment.

#### Working a lot - Waking up energized

The latest trick that I found, which is actually not related to sleep at all, is that working many hours in an intellectually stimulating environment, combined with all of the sleep tricks in this article, leads to an incredible amount of energy in the morning.

The easiest way to explain this is that is similar to multi-day hiking a lot. When hiking 30 km per day for a couple of days in a row, you may feel very tired and sore on the first morning, somewhat okay on the second morning and then super energized from the third day onwards.

Something very similar is happening when your brain is super tired because of a long day of work. The trick here is to avoid late night snacks, alcohol and coffee, and instead invest in a high quality sleep routine.

## My 2025 Routine

Back in 2015, I started reading about the daily routines of celebrities. I found it fascinating and I started incorporating many ideas into my own daily routines. I really tried to be consistent over the years, but every time I would change my country or city, I would hit an invisible RESET button and I would inevitably have to start the routine from scratch. These are my routines for the fall of 2025, starting with the week-day evening, the moment the day begins following through with the morning and then touching on the week-end.

I will talk about bed times and wake up times later in this article, but for now, remember that I start my evening routine at 9 PM, aim to hit the bed at 10 PM,  and wake up between 5:30 AM and 6 AM.

#### Evening Routine

I must have said this a couple of times already, but I strongly believe that the day starts with the evening routine. Getting this wrong means the entire next day can be ruined.

The easiest way to get this right is by starting the routine at 9 PM, despite aiming for a 10 PM bedtime. The most important thing is to get the chores out of the way. That is brushing my teeth and, if it was a sweaty day, have a shower. It is important to do these first because they are mandatory and take time. They also put me in the right headspace for sleep. Brushing my teeth, in particular takes 5-10 minutes with brushing, flossing and polishing with a second toothpaste and electrical toothbrush.

At 9 I also turn off all but one light in the room, generally a lamp that is not directly aimed at my face. That lamp produces enough light that I do not need to turn on the light in the bathroom.

Between finishing the chores and 9:30, I use my laptop to finish any remaining tasks. That can be paying my utilities or rent, reading some low-stakes emails, responding to messages on Facebook or posting the occasional picture. For some reason, ticking off TODO items gets me very cozy, which is helpful with sleep.

During this time, in summer and fall, I turn on the AC to cool down the room. As winter is approaching, that is no longer necessary.

Finally, some days I write down the tasks for the next morning, other days I do not. In the past, I used to also meditate for 10-12 minutes before bed, but I have not reintroduced that to my routine.

Between 9:30 and 10, I head to bed whenever I feel sleepy. These days it's closer to 9:30, but I see no reason to hit the bed and just lay there if I did not get sleepy yet.

Getting to bed means setting my alarm for 6 AM, starting my sleep tracker on Apple Watch and starting the white noise speaker.

#### Morning routine

Assuming everything went well the previous night, by 6:00 AM I should have woken up by myself. Sometimes I wake up at 5:00 AM, other times at 5:30 AM and other times the alarm wakes me up. The earlier I wake up naturally, the better rested I am. Sleeping all the way until the alarm starts (I call it "hitting the alarm") means that something is wrong and I should pay attention to it.

The first thing I do when I wake up is brush my teeth. The freshness of the toothpaste wakes me up. I generally turn on the light in the other room, so that it's still semi-dark in the bathroom and I can slowly get used to the light. Sunrise is 7:14 these days, so there's still about 1 full hour of darkness.

By the time I brushed my teeth, my eyes are ready to see the light, so I turn off my white noise, do my bed, and review the to-dos I wrote the previous night. I have very poor memory and it's normal for me to completely forget what I was doing last night.

Usually, at this point I would have a shower, but recently, the water started making my hands sticky, so what I do instead is writing. Can be either journaling, blogging or writing these words. If I need to get to work early that day, I skip journaling, have a shower and head straight to work, so by 6:15-6:30 I am out the door.

Some things that I used to do in the past were cold exposure exercises. I would either have a cold shower, or, if weather allowed, go outside in 16ºC just in my t-shirt. It improved my immune system during the cold months of the year.

Another thing that I do, mostly in the summers, is go out for a quick walk. The fresh smell of the morning and the sun in my eyes really boosts my mood and my energy.

#### Week-ends

The most important thing on the week-end is that the wake up and bed time stay exactly the same. Because of this, I unfortunately turn down most fun evenings, unless Laura or I really, really want it.

One thing that is different on the week-end is that we try to incorporate some stretching / massage with Laura just before bed, which really helps with getting cozy.

Another thing that I almost always journal on the Saturday and Sunday morning, given that I have no work I need to run to and I almost always go for the morning walk.

## Sleep Experiments

I mentioned that I experimented with bedtime and wake up time to see what best works for me. Here are all of my experiments over the past years and my thoughts on each of them.

I am aware that there are crazier sleep experiments out there, but I always fear that something like that may break me in a way that is not fixable, so I never tried.

#### Fall asleep at 3 AM | Wake up at 11 AM

I did this while in Romania, maintaining a timezone overlap with the 9-5 in California. I would say that it is not as bad as it sounds. Most people go to bed around 1 AM anyway and this is just a few hours later than that. The biggest issue is the 9 - 11 AM interval because a lot of things happen then, particularly people who come to fix stuff around your house.

Mornings were extremely good. For some reason, for me, waking up when the sun is already up is more energizing than waking up in darkness. It is a different type of energy that lasts the entire day.

Ultimately, this was hard to maintain because of the 9-11 interval in the morning.

#### Fall asleep at 1 AM | Wake up at 9 AM

This is an improvement that I've made over the previous schedule when I was in Romania and trying to accommodate work. This fits better with the New York schedule. California can also be fully accommodated from a place like Tenerife or London, the former being actually what gave me this idea in the first place.

This schedule was great in the sense that it very much overlapped normal people's hours and there was nothing ever happening before 9 AM anyway. This broke down whenever I had a late 4PM meeting that I could not reschedule. I would get very sleepy during the meeting and have the next couple of days ruined.

#### Fall asleep at 9 PM | Wake up at 5 AM

I've done this when I had a lot of structure to my day, generally when I'd go to the US for 1 month from Romania. Waking up at 5 AM was very easy because I would have traveled westward from Romania and going to sleep at 9 PM was also easy because I'd be exhausted and I would not take any social offerings anyway.

Ultimately, this is harder to accommodate than 10 PM to 6 AM because a lot of social activities only finish at 9.

#### Fall asleep at any X | Wake up at X+8

Basically, it does not matter when I go to sleep, as long as I get 8 hours of sleep. I used to do this when I was working in London and I had a flexible work schedule. It accommodated social evening events and the occasional dance party. It requires very few sacrifices and it is very sustainable. The downside is that all of the variance in wake up time is not really that great and I'd often have nights when I cannot fall asleep because I woke up too late.

#### Fall asleep at X-8 | Wake up at fixed X

Basically know when I need to wake up and make sure I get 8 hours of sleep in. I had to do this during university, when I'd have lectures at 10 AM and I'd wake up at 9 AM to go make it. This meant I could stay until 1 AM, as long as I got 8 hours of sleep.

This was slightly better because there was less variance in the wake up time (the most important time), but I'd still adjust that to my schedule, so I'd sleep in on week-ends. The downside now was that I'd have to sacrifice some evening plans to make the 8 hours work, but that is a result of my next day's committment and not of the schedule per se.

#### Fall asleep when sleepy | Wake up at fixed X

This is what I was doing during high school. I'd need to wake up at 6:30, so that I arrive to school before 8 and there was no wiggle around that. However, I would only head to bed when I was sleepy.

The downside was that I would often get much less than 8 hours of sleep. However, the body is really good at adapting to less hours of sleep. It's funny, but the body just sleeps more efficiently when it knows it has less hours of sleep available.

In a way, this was probably the healthiest schedule, but the stakes of having a sleepy next day were not as high back then.

#### Fall asleep at 9:30 PM | Wake up before 6:00 AM

This is basically what I do now. I try to be in bed at 9:30 regardless of my sleepiness and I set up an alarm at 6 AM. What happens is that I sometime wake up between 5 AM and 6 AM. If that happens, it means my body is well rested and I get out of bed before the 6 AM alarm. If that does not happen, I am woken by my alarm and I make a mental note of it.

The downside of this approach is that I sometime wake up at 3 AM (for example) ready to go. I check my watch and force myself back to sleep. I think my body is a bit confused about this wake-up-during-last-hour-of-sleep policy, but for now it keeps me super sharp and super energized during the day.

## Fixes from the past

So far, I talked about sleep as if life was perfect and we are all perfect and we can follow routines perfectly and nothing bad ever happens. But that is not how life is. Over time, I had some issues and I solved them. Here is a quick racap of all these issues and how I fixed them.

#### Waking up to drink water

I grew up with a mug next to my bed. If I ever woke up thirsty, I'd drink some water. These days, I replaced my mug with a bottle. What changed however, is that if I wake up thirsty, I force myself to go back to bed. 90% of the time, I'd just fall back asleep and my body would learn that water in the night is not available.

This is harder to do after intense exercise because the body needs more water to recover. In those situations, I do my best to stay hydrated during the day and acknowledge that some water may be necessary during the night, but try to get myself back on track as soon as possible.

#### Waking up to pee

Similar to drinking water, if I wake up and the urge to hit the toilet is not that bad, I force myself back to sleep. The body learns pretty quickly that peeing is not an option and I soon stop waking up for that. I also try to limit my water intake a few hours before bed to help.

This may not be an option for everyone, but for now and hopefully for a while, I am super lucky.

#### Issues falling asleep

In my latest 9:30 to 5:30 sleep routine (week-end included), I haven't really had much trouble falling asleep within 30 minutes. However, all of my previous routines had the occasional insomnia. For me, it would be just one night out of 100, but it was still annoying.

What I found worked best was to call it a work night, get out of bed and work on something, usually on my laptop. Some of my biggest achievements came from this unplanned time I spent on the laptop during the sleepless nights. I would usually need 3-4 hours of work before feeling sleepy, but at the end of it, I would have both achieved something and not stressed over the sleep.

#### Issues waking up

I was usually having these issues when traveling eastward to Romania, after some time spent in the USA. They manifest as a continuous snooze of the alarm and it would get really bad. I'd have my alarm at 9 AM and then snooze it every 10 minutes until 1-3 PM, which, thinking back, is absolutely insane and very unhealthy.

I did not find any solution to this, other than the discovery that being happy made it go away. It could have been some mild form of depression and my sleep was the most receptive to it. As a fix, I would usually try to change my environment or my situation and things would improve quickly, but I realize that I am in the privileged position where I can change either the environment or my day-to-day on a whim.

#### Back pain while sleeping

For a few years, I would wake up with this lower right side back pain. I talked to a couple of doctors and chiropractors, but they could not figure it out. In the end, it got fixed accidentally by changing my mattress and stopping my karting training.

#### Fixing a bad night sleep

Sometime I would wake up still very tired. My trick to fix this is a 1-2 hour nap that happens 1-2 hours after waking up. Basically waking up, going through the routine, realizing that the sleepiness does not go away, and sleep for 1-2 more hours. Anything longer than that will make it harder to sleep the next night and anything sooner than that does not have the desired effect. The key part here is waking up fully and spending 1-2 hours around the house (not sleeping) before going back to bed.

## Bonus section - Sleep after timezone changes

I change timezones quite often, particularly between Romania and California. I got some tips for making it easier to adjust the sleep schedule.

#### Traveling westwards

West is always easier. The trick is to take a flight that leaves in the morning and lands in the evening, do not sleep on the flight at all and go to bed right away at destination. I would usually wake up around 3 AM, but I'd force myself to sleep until 5 or 6 AM. Never nap, then maintain this 9 PM to 5 AM schedule for a couple of weeks.

The downside is that not sleeping on the plane reduces the immune system, so I would generally get sick. To address this, I recently incorporated a short 1-2 hour nap mid flight.

#### Traveling eastwards

Similar trick. Take any flight, but evening is the best. Make sure you arrive at the destination on the evening or late night and aim to go to bed late, but before 3AM. Do not sleep on the plane. Wake up at 11 AM or as late as possible, then stay until 3 AM the next night to get into the routine.

The most important thing here is to never have naps during the day. Without naps, I'd get used to the new timezone in 3-4 days. With naps, it's 2 weeks.
