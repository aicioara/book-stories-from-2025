# Software Engineering Job Levels

Like tabs vs spaces, vim vs emacs and C++ vs Rust, talking about Job Levels is a contentious point. Primarily because the way you climb the career ladder is very different from company to company, but also because career ladders are a made-up concept and because labelling people is generally a very bad idea.

Nevertheless, if you clicked, it means you're interested, so let me give you my take.

First and foremost, I started my career thinking that levels are nonsense. I still believe part of that, but I also believe that different people have different skills and we will nevertheless label people some way or another, so why not use the career ladder for it.

I also really dislike the concept that the ladder should be tied to age. I will make some observations about the expected age, because there is a statistical probability to it, but in my opinion level is only correlated to the level of influence that you have.

Also, people people in a start-up of 20 people or less have a way larger amount of influence and responsibility than even the highest IC level at Google or Meta, so this ladder is for big tech only (think Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Apple, etc).

A very good resource not only for comparing levels, but also salaries is https://levels.xyz.

In terms of naming, I generally use Google's scheme because it is more complete, but your milage may vary.

## The ladder

- Junior Engineer (entry level, recent grad, rotation engineer)
- Mid Engineer (usually called Software Engineer without any other prefix)
- Senior Engineer
- Staff Engineer (tech lead)
- Principal Engineer
- Distinguished Engineer
- Fellow

## Expectations

Here are my expectations for an engineer at each of these levels. Performing above expectations (i.e. at the next level) is reason for promotion.

#### Junior Engineer

Expectations:
- You know how to code (i.e. you don't need to learn)
- You have a degree, either formal or informal
- You understand the tasks and can solve them with some help

Issues:
- You need people to give you tasks
- You need people to help you most of the time with most tasks
- You do not really see the big picture
- You interact mostly with your mentor / a few members of your team
- You are a net negative, as people need to spend time to help you

#### Mid Engineer

Expectations:
- If you receive a task, people trust that you solve it independently
- You are a net positive, no longer need other people for help
- You interact with your entire team

Issues:
- You still don't see the bigger picture or how your work benefits the company
- You do not have responsibility if things go wrong

#### Senior Engineer

Expectations:
- You no longer receive tasks, you receive projects
- You can break projects down into tasks and then solve the pieces yourself
- You lead projects and are DRI if things go wrong
- You mentor Junior Engineers and invest time in helping them succeed

Issues:
- You see the bigger picture, but don't quite understand why the company pursues projects
- You don't have a say in what you work on
- You don't get to tell people what to do

#### Staff Engineer

Expectations:
- You receive bigger projects where the implementation is unclear
- You break projects down into tasks and assign tasks to engineers
- You are trusted with the choice of technology and the direction of the project
- You come up with your own projects and try to get buy in from leadership
- You collaborate with multiple teams to achieve goals

Issues:
- You don't get to set budgets
- You don't get a choice who you get to work with
- You have freedom to pick your projects, but are still expected to complete the projects that are given to you

#### Principal Engineer

Expectations:
- You interact with leaders across the entire company
- You pretty much know everyone and everyone knows you
- You get to decide what you work on and what you do not work on
- You work on cross-company projects where you represent the company
- You set budgets for projects
- You understand the company and what strategic goal every project has

Issues:
- You don't get to interact with the CEO or the SVPs
- You do not set strategic paths for the entire company

#### Distinguished Engineer

Expectations:
- You interact with the CEO and SVPs a lot
- People in the industry know who you are by your name
- You set strategic directions for the company's technology

Issues:
- You may one day leave to be a Distinguished Engineer somewhere else

#### Fellow

Expectations:
- You are basically the company. You are the Jeff Dean of Google, the Jony Ive of Apple. People do not expect you to ever part ways with the company and if you did, a big part of who you are will change.

Issues:
- None

## Variations

Of course, these vary from company to company. For example, some companies add a Senior Staff and Senior Principal position to make things a bit more granular. Other company combine Staff and Principal because they want those people to stop focusing on chasing promotions and start focusing on product. Other companies do not have Fellows, because there is no person noteworthy there. Some companies invert the Staff and the Principal names or introduce a Partner position.

## Conclusion

I hope these help because I will use them when sharing career advice.
