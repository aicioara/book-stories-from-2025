# How I announced my resignation from Apple

## The timeline constraints

When I accepted the offer with OpenAI, on May 15, I was still at Apple and I had two primary constraints:
- A month-long trip to Romania from May 17 to June 15
- A two-week trip to Romania from July 2 to July 20

The first trip had a couple of goals:
- Renew the visas. The $3,000 investment that I mentioned. I would not be able to change jobs before this step, so it was critical that this comes before the move
- Attend Margi's wedding
- Attend Vlad Hategan's wedding
- Go to 2 karting competitions at Brasov

The second trip had three goals:
- Attend the 24h endurance karting race at Brasov
- Celebrate Laura's bday in Greece
- Attend Uca's wedding

Since I would return to USA on July 20 and it is customary (and nice) to give 2 weeks of notice period before leaving Apple, and if I wanted to submit my resignation just after returning to US, resignation should go July 21 (Monday), last day at Apple should go August 1 and first day with OpenAI would be August 4.

However, my salary is influenced by the price of the OpenAI stock and the rule was that the price is dictated by the last day of the month when I joined. If I were to join on August 4, my salary is given by the price of the stock on August 31. If I were to join just one week earlier, the price of the stock would be taken from July 31.

I had a feeling that the stock would go up in August, so it was very important to me to start in July. July 29 (Tuesday) was then chosen as a start date, a decision taken simply on my gut feeling.

I think it is important to actually go into my reasoning to explain the math. The compensation in a tech company has three components: salary, stock and bonus. Salary is the straight-forward payment for the services and bonus is a one-time monetary gift. Stock is the important part because it is the reason working in tech is so lucrative, the price fluctuates with the company valuation. That means, if you join when the company stock is worth $100 and the company is later worth $200, you doubled the stock component of the compensation. If you however join when the company is worth $150 and is later worth $200, you now only increased the compensation by 33%. So joining at a lower number is critical. Also, in a startup, that number only changes once a year. So it would be a huge loss for a single week of delay.

In the end, it turned out that the stock price did not go up in August, so joining in August would have had the same effect as joining in July.

Anyway, coming back from this detour, if I wanted to join OpenAI on July 29, then my last day with Apple had to be July 28, which meant that I had to submit my resignation around July 14, while that my flight to the US was July 20.

This does not seem like a big thing, but it was a HUGE risk. If Apple decided that I would be leaving anyway and they do not want to pay me for an additional two weeks, they could have terminated my employment on the spot, the day I submit my resignation (July 14). If I were outside the USA when I would be fired, then I would lose my visa instantly. By doing so, not only I would lose my job at Apple and the opportunity at OpenAI, but Laura would also lose her job at Google. It was a big gamble.

If however, I delayed it by a week and only given Apple 1 week of notice, then I would have burned bridges and compromised my relationship with Apple. I also have a feeling that the relationship with Apple was important, so I did not want to do that.

So that's the context, let's start the story.

## The final two weeks

The plan was to submit my resignation on July 14 (Monday), but on that day, I was just returning from my 24h karting race and I was super tired. I did not really want to have that conversation while I was tired.

July 15 (Tuesday) was my next opportunity, but it was also the day I went with my parents to a dinner at Nepo Restaurant to celebrate my new job. We got some bottles of wine and I was too drunk when I got home to have any sort of conversation.

July 16 (Wednesday) was the day. I scheduled a 1:1 with Helen, who became my new manager after Ievgen Soboliev left Apple to join xAI. Helen was on holiday when she became my manager, so this would be our first 1:1 ever.

Helen was prepared to welcome me to the team, but I cut straight to the chase and I told her that, even though I loved Apple and even though I was very excited about the future, I unfortunately decided to leave Apple. She was very surpised and asked me what happened. I shared with her that I would be joining OpenAI and she said that "oh, that make total sense". She made it sound like if she had the opportunity to join OpenAI, she would do it as well.

The call was short and focused a bit on logistics. She initially asked me what date I had in mind. I said that I want July 28 to be my last day. She said that that's not exactly 2 weeks and wondered whether I can stay one or two more days, until July 29-30. I stayed firm and apologized for not feeling great the previous days to send the note, but July 28 would be my desired last date.

After the call, I asked Michael Xu for a 1:1 as well. Michael is Helen's manager and I wanted to tell him that Project B, one of the most important strategic bets that I was part of, would need a new owner. The first thing that Michael told me was that he heard that I was planning to leave, so the rest of the conversation circled around that. Michael was a bit more aggressive than Helen. He wanted to see what he could do to keep me around.

By that time, I created a narrative around three main reasons I am leaving Apple. The "paper cuts" and "the straw that broke the camel's back" were of course not part of that narrative. So, when Michael asked me for my reasons, I gave the following three:
- Better pay, even though a big part of it was illiquid stock in a private company
- Better title, OpenAI would make me a Principal Engineer (ICT6 equivalent), while Apple kept me a Senior Staff Engineer (ICT5)
- New opportunities, even though Project B and Project V were important strategic bets for Apple, I was not that excited about them anymore.

Michael wanted to know if having Apple offer more money would convince me to stay. I told him that if Apple offers a "competitive" counter offer, I would of course consider, because, after all, I really liked Apple. If, however, Apple would not be able to even match my OpenAI offer from a comp standpoint, then I would not consider it. I also told him that the most important thing for me is that we stay in good terms, so if my rejection of an eventual retention offer would jeopardise our relationship, then I value my relationship with Michael way more than the counteroffer.

Michael responded that it does not hurt asking for a counter offer, but he doubts that Apple will be able to match OpenAI. This all happened on Wednesday.

On Thursday, Michael got back to me, thanked me for my services and told me that, as expected, Apple would not have the means to match the compensation from OpenAI. He wished me all the best in my new job and asked me to ramp my team up on my existing tasks, so that there will be a smooth transition. I thanked him as well and told him that my team is already almost up to speed and I would spend the following week with them to make sure they have everything they need to be successful. By this time I was still in Romania.

Friday was a day off because I had to attend Uca's wedding. Saturday was a chill day in Romania, when Laura and I went to a movie. Sunday, we almost missed our flight (not our fault) and had to run through the Munich airport with a fractured rib (from karting), then went straight to bed.

(The good thing to note is that Apple did not fire me by this point, so I was able to enter the USA with the new visa and keep my job. My wife too 🙂)

Monday, July 21, one week to go. I finally open my laptop for the first time since talking to Michael on the prior Thursday and my inbox is drowned in messages. Michael tells me that he wants to talk to me because Apple actually wants not only to match, but actually exceed my offer from OpenAI. I also received an email from the assistant of John Gianandrea, our senior vice president, telling me that John wants to meet me personally on Monday.

Good thing that I noticed on time. I responded to all requests and I was scheduled to have a meeting with John after lunch. Initially the call was supposed to be over video, but I realized that I never met John in person and he is quite an important figure, as well as a celebrity. I asked whether I could meet him in person and I was granted my wish, I would finally meet our Senior Vice President in person, and it was going to be just him and I for 30 uninterrupted minutes. I had no idea what he wanted to talk about, but I decided to sketch out a couple of questions.

I am pretty sure that I wrote those questions down on my ReMarkable or on a Trello list, but it looks like I somehow lost the notes. Sad.

I went to Apple Park early to have lunch and then I spent 30 minutes at the reflection pond to gather my thoughts and be ready for the conversation. This is when I believe I wrote those things down, but it looks like I was wrong.

I headed to JG's office early and when I saw he was free, 2 minutes before our scheduled time, I walked in.

JG was awkward as always, which is why I felt the questions that I prepared were necessary. He started the conversation with asking me for the reason of leaving Apple, to which I gave him the same 3 points stated earlier: comp, title and project. He continued the conversation by saying that he and the finance team put together an offer that is supposed to match the one I received from OpenAI, because his goal is to remove the compensation aspect from the conversation. He then said that he could not give me a title bump to match OpenAI, without giving a reason, but, with the right projects, I could be on track to become ICT6 in a year or two, so that should not worry me too much. He then wants to spent the 30 minutes to convince me about item number 3, that the "grass is not greener on the other side" and what sort of projects would I be interested in, so that I can make that ICT5 to ICT6 title jump.

Most of the conversation is a blur to me, especially since today is September 12 and this conversation happened on July 21, almost 2 months ago, but there are a couple of things that stood out to me. One thing was that John was incredibly knowledgeable about the industry.
- He knew exactly how Anthropic and Perplexity were getting their search results that powered their AI search.
- He knew that Tom Gunter left for OpenAI and then immediately transferred to Meta Superintelligence Labs after Ruoming Pang left Apple to go there.
- He mentioned that Tom Gunter and him spoke briefly and Tom mentioned that OpenAI did not have any secret sauce in those models.
- He was super aware of the things Meta was doing at the time, poaching researchers from everywhere for its Meta Superintelligence Labs.
- He said that the Meta effort is silly because they put in charge a 29 year old (Alexandre Wang who was in fact 28). I thought that was very ageist and reiterated my dislike for Apple's internal correlation between age and title.

Since I did not want to start talking about potential projects for me, I started going through the mental list of things I wanted to ask him.
- I asked him how did he take the decision to leave Google for Apple. He said that Google Search became easy technically but hard politically, so coming to Apple was a major relief for him.
- I asked him for career advice and he told me to take risks (which kind of hinted at joining OpenAI TBH).

When I left I told him something along the lines of looking forward to working together more.

Other than this, I forgot most of the stuff. I am sorry I waited so long until this article but the workload at OpenAI was large.

John assured me that I would be receiving a retention offer by the end of the day (Monday). Michael also told me that I would be receiving a retention offer in a day or two. Neither of them knew that they were wrong.

While waiting for the retention offer, I continued my smooth handoff process that I started. I was humbled by the opportunity to talk to John and I was considering the Apple offer if the numbers made sense, but I had a feeling that the numbers (which I did not know at the time) did not make sense.

Tuesday passed. Wednesday passed. Thursday passed. I had yet to see an offer.

On Friday, Helen is asking me if I made up my mind and I told her that I have yet to see Apple's counteroffer. She was surprised. I asked Michael and he told me that the offer was approved by JG on Monday, but it is sitting on Deirdre O'Brian's (our Chief People's Officer and head of HR for the entire Apple) desk right now.

If I wanted to make this story spicy, I could assume that Deirdre was waiting for Tim Cook's personal approval on the compensation package before getting back to me. Realistically, what probably happened was that they were waiting for the one planned review meeting that happened during the week-end.

Regardless, by Friday evening I still did not have a counter offer. I asked Michael what was going on and he said that they are still waiting on Deirdre and he asked me whether I can delay leaving Apple by another week or so. I told him firmly that without an offer, Monday, July 28, would be my last day.

On Friday, I also went to the office, and in doing so, I realized that the entire office was empty (Apple's mandatory in-office days were Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday), but Frank Chu, my director was still in his office.

I stopped by and talked to Frank when I found him free. Frank told me that I am super lucky to get a counter offer from Apple. That would never have happened, but the most recent AI talent poaching spree from OpenAI, xAI and Meta triggered an alarm within Apple and I was the happy beneficiary of that. Indeed, if I were to only use this offer to increase my salary at Apple, without actually considering leaving the company, this would have been a golden opportunity, but by this time, I was already considering leaving.

Frank asked me what I thought of the retention offer and I shared with him that I still did not know the offer or the numbers. He seemed surprised at first, but then he understood and he shared a number with me, verbally. The number would mean an increase in my Apple effective salary. It would, however, not match my OpenAI total compensation.

I was disappointed. After all of this craziness, I would have expected to, at the very least, match the competing offer, or even slightly increase it. At least that is what Michael Xu told me on Monday, just before meeting John.

After running the numbers, I made up my mind that if the offer came back to be that number, I would not accept it. So now, I only had to wait for the actual offer.

Saturday and Sunday passed without any more words and on Monday morning, I received the text from Michael that he finally has an approved offer.
