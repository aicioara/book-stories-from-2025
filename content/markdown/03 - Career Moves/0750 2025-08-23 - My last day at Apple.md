# My last day at Apple

I am writing these words 4 weeks after the fact. Some things may already be mixed up, but for the most part, I think I recall the majority of the facts. I will try to recall as many small details as possible, in the form of a brain dump and maybe later I can turn it into an article or something.

This is a prequel to \[My first day at OpenAI\].

---

I am writing this out of order, but I already talked about my first day at OpenAI, so it is only fair that I also talk about my last day at Apple, while it is still fresh in my mind.

Since I started at OpenAI on a Tuesday, I decided that my last day at Apple will actually be a Monday. This gave me an additional $930.85 gross, and who does not like almost $1,000. It's not like I wanted to do something with that Monday and it just padded my $3,000 visa expense that started all of this.

7 days ago, on the Monday prior, JG gave me a counter offer. However, by the end of the week, I still haven't seen or heard some official numbers. On the Friday prior, I pinged Michael Xu and he told me that it is just sitting on Deirdre O'Brian's desk and should be signed any time soon. He asked me if I could delay my final day with Apple and I told him that if I do not receive that offer, I will just assume it is never coming and my final day with Apple will be Monday.

So Monday is starting. The first thing on my day is a user study at 9:30 AM in a building I've never been in, close to KF04. The week before that, I had a quick user study where they gave me a nice Apple hat and I was excited about more swag or some credits for my last meal at Apple.

As I head to that building, I see a message from Michael Xu telling me that the offer has been approved and that we should meet to discuss. I tell him I'll be back at 11, so we pencil that down.

The user study was really nice. It was about taps and double taps while wearing an Apple Watch Ultra. The asian lady who measured my hand and wrist and everything was really nice, in an ASMR way and the entire study went really well. She offered me a red metallic mug which I happily accepted.

I am heading into Kifer building and I am booking a meeting room that I turn into my office and ask Michael to meet me there at 11. This is an interesting hack, a meeting room that is my own before the meeting and after the meeting is really powerful. Just like going into your boss's office, finding him there and then leaving him there at the end. It's an interesting trick that the brain plays on us. Every time I want to negotiate or seem in a position of power, I should have an office, and booking a meeting room for before and after the meeting achieves exactly that.

Michael walked me through the numbers. I will write them down in the JG counteroffer article, because they fit there better, but, they match the number that Frank Chu told me, which, when I calculated made no sense, so it would have made my life so much easier.

Michael threw in a twist. He told me that the new comp, effective immediately would be Y1. Y1 is smaller than my OpenAI offer, but Y1 grows over time. There are stock refreshers, Apple stock increases 10% YOY, my base comp increases 3% YOY and all of those numbers are conservative. If I project the Y2, Y3, Y4 based on those predictions, then (Y1+Y2+Y3+Y4)/4 will be my OpenAI offer.

Now, there are many problems with this:
1. There is an off-by-one error. Y1 happens in 2026
2. My Apple refreshers will be as large as my cliffs. This was a huge oversight on their part, they haven't considered that stock that I earned in 2022 will finish vesting in 2026 because all our stock vests over 4 years. Apple Stock increased a lot since 2022, so the value of the shares I am no longer earning will be the same as the value of the refreshers that they suggested, resulting in a net-zero
3. They assumed that OpenAI will not grow

What probably happened is that they put these numbers in some internal comp calculator that they have to try to match the OpenAI offer, but that calculator was not carefully considered, so the final 4 year number will be much lower because of the stock that no longer vests.

The biggest problem anyway was the spirit. You need all of these mental gymnastics to make sense of the offer. Just a massive red flag.

The second logistical problem was that, no matter what, even if the stock no longer stopped vesting, even with 20% YOY stock growth, even with 3% YOY base growth, even with bonus, I could not make the numbers match.

I told Michael that I will think about it for 30 minutes and I will get back to him. I wanted to run the numbers and see if my math is wrong somewhere.

I realized that I booked a final meeting with Michael Feng at Apple Park, so I headed there for a quick lunch.

I met Michael and told him that I am leaving Apple. We had the usual open-heart conversation where we gossip about this and that, and eventually we took a selfie by the rainbow sculpture.

Next, I went to SC04 to make sure I took everything because, if I were to reject the offer, they may cut off my access right away and I did not want to complicate my life with belongings in two separate places.

![One last look at my desk at Apple in SC04](attachments/IMG_0837.jpg)

Next, I went home and opened a spreadsheet. I tried really hard to make the numbers match, the way Michael Xu described, but I could not. It also got quite late in the day, so I decided that I am leaving Apple and went back to work.

At 15:00, I shared the news with Michael Xu that I am in fact leaving, then met with Helen Song, my manager for 2 weeks and shared the news with her as well.

Helen thanked me for the 6 years at Apple and told me that there is some paperwork that I need to take care of.

At 16:40, I sent \[Apple Farewell Email\] and I got a couple of messages back on Slack.

Roshni Pradeep was particularly excited about my departure to OpenAI. This other guy who reported to Kevin Hsu wanted to have a WebEx call with me. It was the guy whose team I wanted to join when I eventually picked Ievgen, the guy who was not interested in me and never found a time to meet me to discuss his team. I guess, all of a sudden, he was interested.

I cleaned up my space and had two posters from the RadarCon'25, the Radar Convention. The posters were pretty cute, with the Radar mascot hunting ants (bugs). I did not really need them, so I gave one to Hakesh Darapaneni and one to James McClain. They were not in the office on Friday, so I just left them on their desk with a "Thank you for everything" note.

I also saw that Frank Chu was still in the office, so at 17:30, I went by and shared my farewells. This was the third meeting with him, the first one being right when I joined his org and the second one when I was still deciding if I wanted to stay or go to OpenAI and he tried to share some exciting projects.

This time was different in a sense and identical in much of the rest. We talked mostly about the future of AI. He was very doom-and-gloom about the future of Software Engineering as a job. I was the optimistic counterbalance in the conversation. It is almost a month since that conversation, so I cannot recall a lot, but it felt heart-to-heart, while also somewhat impersonal.

One thing that I had to do was write an official resignation letter. I was unaware of that. I asked ChatGPT to write one for me and it wrote 3 paragraphs. I took a pen and paper and started transcribing it on paper, until I realized that I needed a second sheet because the message was too long and I started from the middle of the page, expecting it to be shorter.

It then dawned on me that I can actually write it in Microsoft Word, print it, sign it and scan it back and it would take me 10 times less time. I did that and I found out that the resignation has to be approved. So I reached out to Helen to approve it, lol.

In a way, I kind of wanted to stay on payroll until they realize that they had to take me off of it. In another way, I did not want to have any visa issues, so I tried to get this right.

I then packed all of my stuff in my car and forwarded a couple of "in case of separation" emails that explained what happens to my benefits and so on. Then, I found a document that explains all the additional steps that I had to do. Things like factory resetting my phones, removing them from iCloud, etc. I started doing that, but then realized it will take me forever, so I just gave up and just removed them from iCloud.

I really wanted to take a picture with all of my devices lined up on a table, so I did that. Helen told me to put them all in a locker and that she will take care of it, so I ended up doing that.

![Dropping off all of my Apple work devices.](attachments/IMG_0897.jpg)



The thing that I was still deciding on was what to keep. I had too much stuff for Apple to track or care. I wanted to keep the old intel-based laptop because I loved it, travelled with it everywhere and because Apple would just throw it away. However, that was tracked, so I decided against it.

In the end, what I kept was:
- My first Apple laptop. The one from Romania with a broken 'e' key. That was not tracked anymore because I was supposed to give it back in 2020 and people did not want to see me due to the pandemic
- One external monitor that was not tracked. It was the one I received on my first day and never removed from the case
- The small iPhone SE 3. Amazing phone and it was rooted, so I could fake my GPS location and check if I still had access to emails
- Keyboard, trackpad and peripherals that I am now using at OpenAI
- The badge. I did not really see a reason to return it.

One of the things that I forgot to do was create an account on teamblind.com using my Apple Email. Oh well.

By the time I finished all of these, it was already late in the night, but I wanted to have one last look at Apple Park, so I headed there on my way home. I walked around Steve Jobs theatre, then around the hills near the park. But the part I liked the most was the reflection pond where I was spending my days when I just joined Apple and where I tried to clear my mind before the meeting with JG.

![Reflection Pond](attachments/IMG_0907.jpg)

I must have spent 30 minutes there, taking some pictures and soaking in the quietness of the place. I walked around the pond a couple of times and once I almost touched some spider web with a Black Widow hanging there. Almost died.

I gave my final farewells, then headed out the gate on Tantau's reception. The gate I used when I first came into Apple to sign my Laserlike -> Apple paperwork, back in July 1st, 2019. WHAT A RIDE!

By the time I got home it was already quite late. My last photo at Apple park was taken at 22:21, so I must have gotten home around 23:00.

I then realized that I haven't finished the Workday assignments for OpenAI, so I started doing that. Of course it was too late, read more in \[My first day at OpenAI\]. I rushed to finish that because I had to wake up at 6:00 the next morning.

My sleep tracking tells me that I spent 6 hr and 3 min in bed that night, of which 5:45 were slept, from 00:13 to 06:00.
