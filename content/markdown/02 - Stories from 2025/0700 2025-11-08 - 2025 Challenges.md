# 2025 Challenges

I have a really big addiction. I wish I didn't. But I had it all my life. I obsessively like to collect things.

From a young age, I loved collecting coins, banknotes, napkins, Pokemon cards, bottle caps, everything. I even went as far as collecting chocolate covers. Anything that is physical. To me, there is something magical about the order and clarity that a **complete** collection gives to me.

I tried really hard to avoid getting into NFT or art or other expensive things, but at my core, I really like collecting stuff.

As I've grown older, I realized that, while I cannot fight this addiction, I can channel it and align it to my goals through a process called gamification. One early example of doing was my challenge to meditate for 10,000 days. The app that I was using, Headspace, would give me digital badges if I maintained streaks of daily meditation.

![Headspace streak](attachments/HeadspaceMeditationStreak.jpg)

I also aligned it with career. I really wanted to code every day and GitHub gives us this convenient calendar where I want to turn every day green. For example, these are my contributions from 2014, the year of my biggest growth as an engineer.

![GitHub contributions from 2014](attachments/Screen%20Shot%202025-11-08%20at%2016.20.19.png)

The thing is that I get really obsessed with making sure that I deliver on these challenges, which can be either good or bad.

For this year, I decided to take two more of these challenges
- Leetcode Daily Challenge
- Strava Monthly Challenge

In this article, I will talk about each.

## Leetcode Daily Challenge

First, a bit of background. https://leetcode.com is a website that prepares software engineers for interviews at tech companies. It is basically a collection of interview questions coupled with an automated evaluator that checks the correctness of solutions.

My goal at Apple was to get a promotion during my time there. I joined as a Software Engineer (ICT3) and I got promoted to Senior Software Engineer (ICT4) within the first year. However, I felt like Apple low-balled me when they hired me. To me, I was supposed to be a Senior Software Engineer when they hired me, and this promotion was not actually an upgrade, it was merely adjusting my position to my correct level. My goal was hence to become a Senior Staff Software Engineer (ITC5). I worked on that for 4 years and in October 2024, I finally received my promotion and hence my job there, as far as I saw it, was done.

Therefore, in October 2024, I started training for interviews again and, to get better at it, I wanted to leverage my collection addiction "loophole". Leetcode has a "daily problem", a problem that they publish daily at midnight. Solving it every day gives us streaks, finishing every problem of the day, the day it is published, gives us badges.

![LeetCode submissions in the past 12 months](attachments/Screen%20Shot%202025-11-08%20at%2016.29.02.png)

The result was that, over the past 12 months, I made 1,095 submissions, solving over 600 problems and getting the monthly badge for December, February, March and April.

And it worked! The 6 months of daily practice got me in a very good spot and helped me land my job at OpenAI, the hottest company in the Bay Area (in 2025) and one of the hardest places to get a job. I received my offer letter on May 19 and I signed it the next day.

However, doing this challenge was far from easy. I am a firm believer that one of the hardest things a human can do is commit to doing an action every single day without missing a day. Do it through good times, but also through holidays, through parties, through sickness, through emergencies, and so on.

There are two memories that I have from this challenge.

One was December 1, 2024, when I was on my last day in India. I solved the problem and wrote the code for the solution from my phone. Because I could not make the indentation to work properly, I could not use Python, my favorite language, so I had to switch to C++ and write a 1-liner, so that I wouldn't need to deal with the lack of ergonomics on my phone.

The second one was January 18, 2025, when I was in Death Valley. I did not own an iPad at the time, so I still wrote it on my phone, but this time, I also brought a bluetooth keyboard, so that I can actually code properly. In this picture, I am using the hotel writing pads to solve the problem, my iPhone 11 Pro as a display and my Apple Bluetooth Keyboard to write the solution. I solved two of the problems, but on my third day, the problem was too hard and I failed the streak.

![Me trying to solve a LeetCode problem while on holiday](attachments/IMG_9596.jpeg)

![Setup to write code on my phone](attachments/IMG_9591.jpg)

The stretch goal for all of this, besides my addiction and the interview preparation, was to get a t-shirt. You see, every day you solve a problem, you get 10 coins. You need 7,200 coins to buy a t-shirt, so 720 days in total, but there are various bonuses for streaks.

The prediction, if I kept my streaks past May, was that I was going to get the t-shirt on November 10, which is in two days. However, once I broke out of the streak, the addiction was gone and it became hard to get myself to solve problems after already spending 14+ hours a day at work. Right now, I am sitting on 5,113 coins, so I am 2,087 coins short of my goal, but I will continue solving the occasional problem until I (hopefully) get there.

## Strava Monthly Challenge

https://strava.com is an application that one can use to track physical activities such as running, swimming, cycling, hiking and so on. The way it works is that, whenever I start an exercise, I would record my route, pace and heart rate using my Apple Watch Series 5 and, at the end, I would upload the content to Strava.

I used Strava for quite some years already, starting from my conversation-opening cycling journey from London to Paris. Just like Leetcode, Strava also has this concept of gamification in the form of trophies. There are trophies for many things, but the one I was interested in was the 50 km / month hiking challenge. Basically, if I complete 50 km of hiking between January 1 and January 31, I would get a trophy, and so on.

The reasoning behind this challenge was to keep myself fit. We often get busy with work and we forget to take care of our bodies. This challenge would force me to keep moving.

Just like the Leetcode challenge, Strava also proved to be very hard. 50 km of hiking, even at a 5 km/h pace (very fast), is still a 10 hours per month commitment. Commuting to and from the hike would add at least that another 10 hours on top. Combined with the rest of my activities: the writing this book, working 14+ hours a day, the Leetcode challenge and spending time with my wife, I would often get to the last day of the month with 30km still to go.

However, even in those instances, I would not quit. I would hike 30 km in a single day, often solo, and in doing so, I completed the streak for the first 11 months of the year.

![Strava achievements](attachments/IMG_2082.jpg)

To make this even more difficult, I made it a rule to always have the "picture of the hike": one photo that I take during the hike, over-edit it, and post it to Strava along with my route.
