# iPhone

I think I will be buying the new iPhone 17 Pro. That may not sound like a big deal for you, but it is a big deal to me. In this article, I will get into my reasoning.

## iPhone 11 Pro

During my 6 years at Apple, friends and family always asked me what I thought about the latest iPhone and whether they should buy it. One of the controversial things that I said that really triggered people was that, in my opinion, the iPhone is the cheapest phone on the market. My argument is that an iPhone will outlast any Android phone out there, so the price per usage is actually much lower than an Android phone, even though the absolute price of the iPhone is greater than the absolute price of the Android.

One of the reasons an Apple device will outlast an Android device is that software updates will continue to arrive for older Apple devices, while Android devices were supported (or at least used to be supported) for around two years. So, in my argument, if you keep an Apple device for 6 years and you change your Android device every 2 years, over a 6 year horizon, as long as the iPhone is less than 3x the price of the Android, the iPhone is cheaper.

I felt very strong about that and no one ever agreed with my position, so I decided to put my money where my mouth is. In September 2019, three months after I joined Apple, I purchased a fully specced iPhone 11 Pro with my own money. Today, it is September 2025, and I kept the phone for 6 years and it has been my daily driver for all of this time. The Maximum Capacity of my battery still shows `95%` and it still lasts the entire day. I could keep it for another couple of years without any issue.

When I purchased the device, it costed $999. Given I kept it for 6 years, that averages out to $166 per year, or about $14 per month. This is a device that I use every single day, take with me everywhere, I use it to navigate, to pay for things, to open my front door, to stay on top of news and to communicate with friends.

On top of that, the iPhone retains value much better than any other phone. Even now, in 2025, this 6 year old phone can be sold for $252 (Amazon) or $233 (Craigslist). Let's say I want to sell it quick for $150, that means the price for the phone was in fact $850, which came down to $11.80 / month over 6 years. Just as much as a Spotify subscription.

Even with the 512 GB of storage that I actually got ($1350) and with 10% sales tax ($135) and without selling it for at the end (-$150), a scenario closer to my actual situation, the phone was as cheap as $21 / month.

If the price of the iPhone was between $11.80 and $21.00 / month, depending on your situation, the equivalent (full) price of an Android phone that you only keep for 2 years would need to be between $283.20 and $504.00. Show me a luxury phone as cheap as $504.00, or a regular phone as cheap as $283.00 and I will stand corrected.

Anyway, detour aside, the point that I was trying to make is that I kept my 512 GB iPhone 11 Pro as my daily driver for 6 years, it still rocks and it is still very much usable.

## iPhone 17 Pro

Alright, so why change it then?

Another thing that I hear very often is that Apple stopped innovating and that every phone is just marginally better than the phone in the previous year, just better battery and better cameras.

If you look at it that way, it could be true, from one year to the next, the step change is small. But that is NOT how you should change your phone. Apple actually bets on the fact that you change your phone every 2 years.

However, here is a new experiment. These are the things that changed from iPhone 11 Pro to iPhone 17 Pro. In other words, here are all the improvements that someone that kept the phone for 6 years would get if I were to switch to the newer model.

## Samsung Galaxy S4

I kept that phone for a bit less (5 ¼ years), but at the time of exchanging it, the phone was 6 years old, so the time step was just as big as the iPhone 11 Pro -> iPhone 17 Pro. ## Conclusion

I just want to reiterate that
1. The iPhone is the cheapest phone out there if you upgrade once every 6 years.
2. Apple produces innovation every year, it is just very hard to see because the steps are too small year-over-year.
