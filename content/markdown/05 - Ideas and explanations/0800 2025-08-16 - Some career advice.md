# Some career advice

In the wake of my move from Apple to OpenAI, and because I opened my profile using LinkedIn premium, I received a couple of asks for career advice on LinkedIn. I want to share here a couple of the responses that I gave there.

---

## Tips and suggestions for how to get noticed by recruiters

The tip is that recruiters don't really notice. They just pursue the list that the hiring manager sends to them. And the hiring manager takes recommendations from his or her team. Apple is a big cross functional org. Be helpful to people and make a lot of friends. Then, when friends go to OpenAI, or anywhere you like, ask them for a referral. If they refuse, it means you haven't been helpful enough to them, so try harder with the next batch.

Also, always do your best work. People seem to notice.

## General advice to get to OpenAI

In terms of general advice, do a lot of coding every day, be obsessed about the craft, work on communication skills, always be positive and make a lot of friends in the industry.

## Romanian advice for getting to OpenAI

Things that helped my changes of getting into OpenAI.

It helped that I had relevant experience for exactly the role that they were looking for. It also helped that they were hiring aggressively and had a lot of headcount. Finally, it helped that I am writing code every single day and have been writing code almost every single day for the past 20 years. Other than that, I'd say that it was mostly luck.

## Skills vs noise

> Congrats on the new role. I’m curious what ML infra skills were top signals for OpenAI compared to the “noise” that everybody says are “top skills” to have

I cannot speak on behalf of OpenAI and every team here looks for something different in the talent they hire, but as a general rule of thumb, all employers are looking for 3 things. In the order of importance
1) Personal reputation
2) Ability to code, even blindfolded
3) Excellent communication skills

Bonus, if you're researcher, many citations on your papers.
