# iPhone 17 - First Impressions

Alex Solot, a good friend of mine, visited the Bay Area yesterday. He was a bit disorganized with the planning, but insisted to bring his manager (also Alex) along, so I also brought Laura along. We planned to go visit Muir Woods, but then changed to Santa Cruz, which was closer, then changed again to Alviso which was even closer, which is where we went. After a quick walk, they got hungry, so Laura wanted to go to the Google Visitor Center to grab an early dinner. Since we were there, I proposed to go and also visit the Apple Visitor Center, so that we can also see the new iPhones.

I wrote earlier about the latest \[iPhone\] and the fact that I am considering upgrading my 6 year old iPhone 11 Pro with something new. Since I was there yesterday, I wanted to capture my initial thoughts while they are still fresh. So here it goes.

First of all, what impressed me was the store itself. The line was wrapping around the building. We initially thought that was the line to get into the store, similar to the one in Dubai, but instead it was the line to pick up the phone for a particular time slot that people picked. Turns out that the iPhone is still strong in sales, which I personally expected. Both the iPhone (17?) Air and the iPhone 17 Pro are different from last year's models.

Despite the line to pick up the phone, you could go inside to check out the phone, which is what we planned to do anyway. I don't think I've ever seen the store as packed as it was today. I could barely navigate between the tables (the store itself is large and should have plenty of room on a regular day). I did manage to see: iPhone 17 Pro, iPhone Air, the new iPhone cases, the new Apple Watch Bands. Here are my thoughts.

## iPhone 17 Pro

This is the phone that I am thinking of buying. It comes in three colors: Silver, Navy and Orange. They call them Silver, Deep Blue and Cosmic Orange, but that is just marketing. All three colors are stunning. Orange is very popping and it looks like a pumpkin, but its second tone (on the ceramic part of the back) does not really color match. Navy looks the most stylish and is the closest replacement to the Space Gray that they no longer produce. Silver is exactly like color of my MacBook, yet the second tone is a bit too white.

Out of all three colors, I think silver is the best pick because it matches the color of the aluminum the best. Something I've noticed with the Space Black MacBooks is that the color was only applied to the surface of the metal. That means, if you scratch it, the shiny silver of the aluminum will pop out. I think an iPhone is a lot more prone to scratches than a MacBook and I think Aluminum is a lot softer than glass, stainless steel or the titanium that they used before, meaning it will absolutely scratch. Silver is the one that will make this the least noticeable.

The first thing that I notice, holding the phone in my hand is that the UI is incredibly quick and the 120 Hz display really helps it feel quicker. This thing alone makes me consider an upgrade. I want a phone that can move as quickly as I think, so that it encourages me to think even faster. I tried the home screen and the settings, then a little bit of browsing. Safari preloads the website as you type (without showing you), so navigating the web also feels instantaneous.

The new Liquid Glass UI that is shipping in iOS 26 is not as noticeable, which is a good thing. There are a few artifacts here and there, but unless I looked for them specifically, I may as well have used the previous iOS. However, during the 2 minutes I held the phone I did notice a couple of UI bugs which I really hope will be ironed out as new releases roll out.

I also took a browser performance test with [Speedometer 2.0](https://browserbench.org/Speedometer2.0/). This is a yearly tradition that I started with my first MacBook Air, back in 2018. I have to find the numbers for older models, but I just ran the benchmarks again on a couple of the devices that I found around the house.

Notes:
- MacBooks run on a Chrome profile with no extensions and on Safari with no extensions
- iPhones all run on Safari unplugged
- iPads run on Safari plugged in
- I keep running the test until the error rate converges and stabilizes

Now, these numbers appear to be very far from my memories, I remember iPhone 11 Pro was around 135, but my memory is not to be trusted. The idea is that the iPhone 17 Pro is about twice as fast on a single thread. I also tend to believe the 3.1 benchmark more.

Last thing that I tried is the camera. The new addition in this year's model is the 48 MP, 4x Telephoto lens, which is capable of 8x Zoom @ 12 MP. So that is exactly what I tried. I was super disappointed by the result, which looked grained by a lot of noise. What I suspect happened is that Apple automatically  switched from the 4x lens to the 1x lens because there was not enough light. It does that with the 2x lens on the iPhone 11 Pro sometimes as well and it is annoying. I definitely have to test again, but if I cannot get a good shot, then this phone is not really worth the money, because my primary driver for an update is the 4x telephoto lens.

## iPhone Air

I then moved on to the next table to check out the Air. One of the confusing thing about this model is that they called it the iPhone Air, not the iPhone 17 Air, so we will go with that.

The iPhone Air comes in 4 colors: Sky Blue, Light Gold, Cloud White and Space Black, but the Blue, Gold and White look almost identical. Next to each other, you can tell which one is which, but in isolation it is impossible.

As I expected, the iPhone Air feels great in hand. I would say that the fact that it is lighter is more impressive than its thickness, but probably, what feels really good is the ratio of screen to weight. In other words, it is more impressive that it is both large and light at the same time. In fact, I just checked and, at 165 grams, the iPhone Air is heavier than the 144 grams of iPhone SE3, my all-time-favorite phone.

As I expected, the phone is slightly heavier at the top than it is at the bottom, so it feels a little bit unbalanced, but other than that, it is perfect. It looks and feels like a piece of very expensive jewelry. For the first time ever, I was very impressed by the wallpaper, which appear to complement the thinness of the device and made the entire thing feel more premium.

I think the reasons it feels so premium are:
- Titanium for weight
- Polish on the titanium
- Glass on the back for better feel and better grip
- 120 Hz for better looks.
- Nerfing of pros by removing polish (a few years ago) and glass back (this year).

Basically, every single thing about the exterior of this phone is incredibly beautiful and feels very expensive. I expect people who buy a phone for the status symbol will go for this one.

The phone also felt incredibly fast, probably because of the 120 Hz display but also maybe because, without thermal capping, since I just picked it up, the CPU was allowed to run at full speed.

The camera app felt weird because it only had the 1x and 2x display options. So definitely do not buy this phone for its photo taking abilities.

What I told Laura was "If I did not care about the utility and only cared about the looks, the iPhone Air is definitely the phone that I would go for. It is, by far, the best looking iPhone". Laura sarcastically responded "Of course... why would I buy a phone for its utility... I always wanted to cary a piece of metal in my pocket just to look at it."

## iPhone 17

This is a phone that I haven't had a chance to see. There were too many people and I was way too uninterseted to have a look. From what I can tell, it's an iPhone 16 in different colors with slightly better CPU.

Now... that's not to mean it is not a good phone. In my opinion iPhone 16 was a HUGE step from iPhone 15. It felt like in 2024, Tim Cook decided to tell the entire planet to stop buying Pros and just buy the regular phone instead. Other than 1 missing camera, iPhone 16 was almost identical to the iPhone 16 Pro. The fact that iPhone 17 is just an faster iPhone 16 is a massive compliment.

I am just not that interested in that model, so I skipped.
