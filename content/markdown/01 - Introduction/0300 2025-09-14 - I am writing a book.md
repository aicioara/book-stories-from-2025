# I am writing a book

When I think about adult life, I see 2 parallel tracks. They start the moment we enter school and they continue indefinitely for the rest of our lives. They are the **work life** and the **recreational life** and they each take about a third of our living hours, the final third being sleep. How we decide to spend each half varies from person to person and it created many schools of thought over the years, all of which fall under the work-life balance umbrella.

The work life starts in school (or maybe kindergarten) and continues through University and later through career. This life is usually forced upon us. We get some leeway in the sense that we can steer it left and right slightly, but for the most part, we do not have much say over how we spend these hours. They are a necessity and they often give us purpose and a sense of identity. It is the foundation that allows the recreational life to happen.

The recreational life, for the most part also starts in school. It is the life that you have once you get back from school and you go play football with your friends. It is the glass of wine that you enjoy with your wife after a long day of work. We get a lot of freedom to live this life however we please. In fact, we get too much freedom, so much that many people do not know how to use it, which is why we tend to gravitate towards the work life (career) to find a sense of purpose and identity.

I personally enjoy the recreational life a lot because I really like to add structure and purpose to it. While I have almost no control over my time at work, I get to structure my time at home around projects that I generally timebox into 3- or 6-months sprints. In corporate speak, those would be 1- and 2-quarter projects. This style of thinking fits my mental model because I like things to have a beginning and a finality. In other words, I like finite projects.

To give some examples from the past, Q4 of 2020 was dedicated to learning about real estate investing, Q1 of 2021 was dedicated to buying my first apartment and settling down. Q2 and Q3 of 2021, the project was buying a run-down apartment and managing a team of handymen and designers to create a beautiful AirBnB. Q4 of 2021 was dedicated entirely to building a relationship with my new girlfriend (now wife), and so on. More recently, Q1 2025 was dedicated to settling down in the USA - buying a car, renting a place. Q2 2025 was dedicated to changing my job from Apple to OpenAI.

For Q3 and Q4 of 2025, I am working on a very exciting new project. A life-long dream of mine and something I wanted to do for a very long time. While my work life will be absorbed by OpenAI and all of the excitement there, my recreational life will be dedicated to writing a book. Hopefully the book you are reading right now.

In fact, calling it a book is an exaggeration. The only book-y thing about this is that you are (hopefully) reading these words out of something that looks and feels like a book. The content is a collection of short stories that would otherwise have landed on a Blog somewhere on the World Wide Web. This is an experiment into understanding what it takes (and how long it takes) to write a book. It is an exercise towards a second book that I want to write. I want to take all the learnings from this one and poor it into that one.

However, this will be my first book ever, so all I am trying to say is **thank you**! Thank you for reading my first ever book and please keep in mind that my writing can only get better from here. I hope there will be many more to come.

## What this book will cover

These are my stories from 2025, written in a personal-diary style, most of them written as-they-happened. 2025 was a pivotal year for me, both personally and professionally. On a personal plan, Laura and I finally moved together to the United States. On a professional plan, I changed my job from Apple to OpenAI, after 6 amazing years spent at the company.

You will find here a collection of personal stories and philosophical opinion pieces about the way I see the world, especially in the wake of the Artificial Intelligence revolution. Keep in mind that stories and opinions are much easier to get right in hindsight, and while most of these stories made sense at the end of 2025, they may no longer make sense whenever you read them, later into the future.

This book was intended to be published on Christmas 2025, but in reality, the deadline to stop writing is mid-November 2025. Because I want to cover one full year, this book will cover the time from October 2024 to October 2025.

I hope you enjoy reading this very first book.
