# I purchased a new carry-on bag

When I moved to California in 2015, I received three keys. One was my front door, one was my mailbox and the third was a storage room in the basement. One day, I decided to check out the storage room and I discovered that it was absolutely filled with items from the previous tenant. I did not really need that space anyway, left it untouched, but one thing caught my eye. A 22 inch carry-on suitcase from Riccardo Beverly Hills. Despite its name, it is not an expensive brand. I really liked it because it had a tag that said "Hawaii Inspected" from one of its previous trips, so I adopted it as my own.

I also did not have a suitcase this size. It is one of those you can take with you on board of an airplane, but you have to stow away in the overhead compartiment. 

When I fly, I really dislike checking in bags to hold. Having them lost is very disruptive (and very likely), waiting in line to drop it off is annoying, waiting at the carousel to pick it up is wasteful and, worst of all, most can only survive a couple of flights in those harsh conditions before breaking. As an alternative, I always travel with my carry-on, making it my most important travel item. 

When I picked up this used suitcase from the underground storage room, I had no idea that it was going to become my most dear travel accessory.

For the following 10 years, this suitcase has followed me everywhere. It joined me on my month-long trips through South America and South East Asia and it fit all of my remaining belongings in 2020 when I decided to leave California and move to Hawaii. It served as my primary wardrobe during the months I was living from my suitcase in Cluj Napoca and, at its peak, it stowed all of my clothes for beach, party, hiking, business and leaisure for the month I was digital nomading in Tenerife.

Occasionally, the airport staff would force me to check it in because they claimed it was too big to fit in the overhead compartiment (it was not, but ok), and every time I did that, something would break. First it was the handlebars, then the wheels, then the plastic shell. In one unfortunate winter day, while I was pulling it up the cobblestone in Arosa during a skiing trip, one of the wheels fell off and it was lost forever. I kept fixing the occasional problem, but in the end it became obvious that it reached and far exceeded the intended lifespan, so after 197 flights together, I had to let it go.

I tend to take great care of my items and to replace them as rarely as possible, usually with identical copies. Unfortunately, the manufacturer no longer produced this exact suitcase. It produced others, but, in my opinion, they were greatly inferior to my dear bag. This is how I started a multi-week, obsessive search for the perfect carry-on replacement. I will share my findings here, none of the links are affiliate.

The market appears to have consolidated in a limited number of options. Here are the main things you can choose from.
1. **Style**: Hard Shell and Soft Shell. Soft Shell is, in my opinion greatly superior because the material lasts longer, scratches are not as visible, and they have a bit of stretch tolerance for the occasional extra t-shirt. Hard Shells are also always clam style, which means that you usually fill in each half independently and you need more space when opening it up, making it less ergonomic when you do not have a wardrobe and have to live out of your suitcase
2. **Size**. You generally want to go for the biggest size that the flight allows. That would be a 22" for US domestic flights and 21" for international flights. I spent some time creating a table with every single airline and their max carry-on size limits. 
3. **Wheels**. You can choose between spinner wheels and fixed. Fixed will last you much longer, but they are a pain to operate on planes, busses and airports. I recommend spinners.
4. **Quality**. You usually care about three main pieces: zippers, overall material and wheels. Zippers are the most important if you overpack because they are the first ones to fail. Here, the bigger, the better because it makes it easier to close the luggage. Also if you see YKK written on them, they will last you a lifetime. For the material, if you go for a shoft shell, you need ballistic nylon. If you go for hard shell... I don't know. Plastic is lighter but breaks easily. Aluminium is popular now, but it is much heavier and you might have an issue with the scale. Wheels also come in various quality levels. Usually, two wheels per leg (8 wheels total for spinners) are better. There are also silent wheels that have better structure and will last much longer. Unfortunately, they are all plastic.

I started my search by asking a few friends on Instagram. Starting from these, I continued with my own research and I found the following. 

I obviously started from **Riccardo Beverly Hills**. They only sell two soft-shell carry on suitcases now:
- https://www.amazon.com/Ricardo-Beverly-Hills-Sustainable-Eco-Friendly/dp/B0CX3HXJCB
- https://www.amazon.com/Ricardo-Beverly-Hills-Spinner-Luggage/dp/B09R3QRXXK

Feedback:
- Too small: I was looking for 21-22 inches, instead of 20 inch
- Zipper too small compared to the previous generation
- Felt cheap and poor quality

I continued my search with **SwissGear**, which I found discounted at a Nordstrom Rack:

- https://www.amazon.com/SwissGear-Softside-Luggage-Spinner-21-Inch/dp/B08KYG83TG

Feedback:
- The SwissGear felt a lot more premium. I actually ended up buying it and later returning it
- The main issue was that it looked really big and it fit very few items
- The second main issue was the expansion zipper. It only expanded on 3 sides, creating a wedge-shape which did not help much, while destabilizing the entire suitcase and making it look much bigger

I then looked into **AWAY**, which was recommended as "everybody who is somebody has this suitcase".
- https://www.awaytravel.com/products/softside-bigger-carry-on-navy-blue

Feedback:
- Away appears to be mainly focused on the design. Those things indeed look really beautiful
- However, I do not care about the looks here. I want to keep it for another 10 years and it will look damaged in a year or two anyway, so I'd rather have it practical than beautiful
- It felt too fragile

I then found **TravelPro**. I looked at two models:
- https://www.amazon.com/Travelpro-Lightweight-Rugged-Backpack-laptop/dp/B07FX2QZVX
- https://www.amazon.com/Travelpro-Platinum-Softside-Expandable-Spinner/dp/B07XSBGH1G

Feedback:
- TravelPro has by far the best value to price ratio. It is an above-average suitcase for below-average price
- However, I wanted something that was excellent and the price was not my top consideration
- Eventually, just like the SwissGear alternative, I did not like its wedge shaped expansion and I did not like that it had too many compartiments inside. I wanted something much simpler

I then found **TUMI**:
- https://www.amazon.com/Alpha-International-Access-Wheel-Carry/dp/B07MQWDTSX

Feedback:
- TUMI is a really expensive brand. Far above everything else on the market
- I ended up not buying it because, despite price not being a consideration, TUMI did not have anything that made it stand out. The same materials, designs and ergonomics existed on other cheaper brands and I felt like for TUMI, you are only paying for the brand.

Then I tried the **Briggs and Riley**:
- https://www.amazon.com/Briggs-Riley-ZDX-Expandable-Luggage-Spinner/dp/B0873S4BV4

Feedback:
- What I really liked about Briggs and Riley is that they have a lifetime waranty. I haven't had a chance to use it yet, but they claim that any wheel falling off will be replaced for free, which is exactly what I am looking for. I believe they rely on the fact that people will want a newer suitcase in a few years, but I really want something that I can use the rest of my life, while repairing the occasional wheel falling off
- The suitcase expanded on all 4 sides, which was exactly what I was looking for
- After purchasing it, I did a test against my SwissGear and it fit a lot more clothes inside
- The dimensions were much bigger than the rest of the suitcases considered

So, in the end, I settled on the Briggs and Riley and have been using it since November 2024. Here are a few things that I like and dislike about this bag:
- While they say that the exterior handlebar is a feature, I do not agree. Having the handlebars inside did not cause any issues because the clothes would fold around the material. On the contrary, having them outside makes the bag look bigger and had to sacrifice some internal space in order to fit into the carry on dimensions
- B&R has a more expensive version (https://www.amazon.com/Briggs-Riley-Spinners-Baseline-Essential/dp/B09Y2B3WMG) which is much better because it can expand and later compact the clothes. I checked that system live at a shopping center a few months after I purchased mine. I liked it much better because it would fit a lot more clothes, but it also adds a bit of extra weight. At that point, it was too late for a change anyway.
- The wheels are very good. To the point where it keeps running around in a bus. I wish the front wheels had some breaks I could trigger.
- The handlebar is very good and is designed to make it easier to load and unload backpacks that fit on top of the suitcase. 
- However, it is still not as good and not as spacious as my previous Riccardo Beverly Hills suitcase.
- Each suitcase has a unique ID number that you can register, so if it is ever found, and you are lucky, you may be contacted.
- The suitcase is now on sale, which makes me think that I've made a bad deal when I purchased it.
